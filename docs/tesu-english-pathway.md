# TESU English proposed course pathway

The current [English degree](https://tesu.smartcatalogiq.com/en/current/undergraduate-catalog/degree-programs-and-certificates/bachelor-of-arts-programs/bachelor-of-arts-in-humanities-disciplines/english) is represented by a complete 120-credit ledger: 45 general education, 45 area of study and 30 unrestricted electives. Required English courses retain their published identities. Six named courses are editable proposed choices within the 18-credit English-elective allowance; they are not described as an expressly mandated six-course list. Current undergraduate English and Literature course definitions establish the chosen subjects, quantities, levels and online availability. No old catalog is used as current approval.

The baseline selects ENG-2010 (with ENG-2020 as an alternative), ENG-2050, LIT-2210, LIT-3010, LIT-3020 and LIT-3310. Four separately allocated Humanities choices are ART-1000, HUM-1030, PHI-2860 and MAN-3730, each present on the university's [Humanities list](https://www.tesu.edu/degrees-programs/courses/general-education/2021-and-after.php). The existing conditional Study.com Business 324 award for MAN-3730 may be reused only with its original receiving-school conditions. No new English-specific equivalency is inferred. The 18-credit upper-level minimum applies inside the area of study, including LIB-4950; upper-level free electives cannot replace it.

Formal ENC-1020 prerequisites are retained for LIT-4600, LIT-3010, LIT-3020 and LIT-3310. Composition and introductory-literature advisories are distinct from formal prerequisites. LIT-2910 retains its published 2000-level code despite informal upper-level wording in its advisory. The capstone's near-completion advisory does not create a fabricated numeric admission threshold.

ENG-2010 and ENG-2020 retain distinct course identities and university options within one English-elective allocation. Their sourced no-double-credit condition is included as `courseCreditExclusions`; this condition must also be enforced when existing coursework could otherwise enter another allocation. No course-code alias is invented to merge the two original courses.

The published SOC-2910 and HIS-1140 general-education choices avoid a TECEP-only PHI-1300 fallback. All new English selections offer online coursework; there is no compulsory TECEP-only English choice. University-only SOS-1100 and LIB-4950 remain separate from the standard 15-credit eligible-course residency. The derived 105-credit outside ceiling does not override the combined 90-credit alternative-source maximum or establish an actual 105-credit transfer basket. C minima remain scoped to composition and the area of study. A business-specific currency policy is not copied into this B.A.

The source-bound current-catalog identity does not invent an academic-year label. Existing TESU transfer awards retain their original evidence dates and conditions; the importer creates no new provider equivalencies. Published standard tuition remains a provisional planning basis, with fees, books and provider subscriptions separate.
