# Two additional automatic Liberty pathways

This release adds complete, source-reviewed requirement ledgers for Healthcare Administration B.S. online and Psychology B.S. residential. Each can be selected from the existing Liberty degree family, opens a proposed 120-credit course plan, reuses the student's original learning records and participates in comparisons. The registry grows from 39 to 41 compiled models, alongside the four established models.

Healthcare uses 42 general-education credits, nine major foundations, 57 major-core credits and 12 free electives. It retains the three-credit UNIV 104 case and both Bible requirements rather than assuming individual waivers. The model enforces 30 university credits, 30 upper-level credits and 25% of the 66-credit major, with C minima scoped to upper-level major requirements.

Residential Psychology uses 37 unique general-education credits, 12 foundations, 39 major credits and 32 electives. BIOL 102 and PSYC 101 also meet six general-education credits; they are counted once in the degree ledger and displayed as shared requirements. The route retains positive-credit INFT 110 and the residential PSYC 497 selection. It preserves CSER obligations, source prerequisites, grade rules and overall/major residency. An online capstone is not substituted.

## Transfer evidence

A separately maintained supplement records 51 additional named provider-to-receiving-course matches across 31 receiving codes, sourced from six online/residential university guides for Sophia, Study.com and StraighterLine. These are conditional planning candidates for the exact degree and catalog. They are not 51 new degree credits, guaranteed purchases, individual approvals or a new maximum-transfer claim.

The compiler can reuse these awards only for the same school, undergraduate level, receiving credit quantity and reviewed delivery format. University-only rows remain protected. Elective options retain department/level restrictions and duplicate-credit checks. A guide's receiving credit award does not establish independent provider-credit quantities; those stay unknown unless separately sourced. The library does not invent them. Proposed-plan comparisons retain unknown original quantities, while still showing the receiving award. Progress recording asks for a saved original course quantity and credit units before it can create or update a completion record for these guide-only options.

Source generation and its read-only checks are part of the production build. Curriculum review dates, guide publication dates, receiving awards and source-course facts remain separate. All facts are preloaded; this adds no per-student AI service.

## Prices and preferences

The new programs use explicit reviewed tuition bindings with exact school, format, semester units, total, catalog and source checks. Online per-credit billing and residential full-time/part-time billing follow the existing tuition engine. Unconfirmed fees and transfer policies stay visible; financial comparisons are provisional. Numerical academic conflicts continue to block estimates.

Fresh plans consider the four reviewed providers. A saved provider selection, including an empty or narrow selection, is preserved. Proctoring preferences still apply. Source records and school-specific evaluations are retained without turning another program's approval into an approval for these programs.

## Validation

New tests exercise both real curricula, guide scope and unknown source quantities, course-section progress, duplicate/shared credits, tuition bindings, existing-course reuse, and explicit provider preferences. Existing Journey, curriculum, savings, comparison and Worker tests remain part of verification. Browser rendering is not claimed: this environment's Chromium previously failed at startup, so executable planning and transport checks are used here.

Coverage remains specific. The online Information Technology variants need a separately resolved cognate-residency rule before they can be published safely. Other listed degrees without complete source evidence remain visible as research gaps.
