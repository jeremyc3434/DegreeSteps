# Cost and speed comparisons

Research checked November 2, 2026 against the official 2026–27 university schedules and the dated October 2026 WGU catalog. `dist/data/cost-comparisons.json` contains seven examples from six universities. Liberty residential and Liberty online are separate attendance examples, not two different colleges.

## What the numbers mean

The comparison is **university tuition plus an editable outside-credit budget, before financial aid or discounts**. WGU's listed term amount also includes its required $200 resource fee; that difference is stated beside its fees. The other rows exclude additional school fees, materials, housing, food, travel and living costs. They are planning illustrations, not degree-price quotes, an offer of admission, or an approved course basket.

The baseline assumes all 120 reference credits/CUs are completed at the university. The transfer scenario assumes the stated outside units actually apply to the chosen degree. Original-source learning, university evaluation, major/residency rules, prerequisites, credit age and course availability still control the real plan. A comparison does not reimburse past tuition or establish how much aid a particular student will retain after transferring.

All modeled university work uses the currently published rate on both sides. This holds current rates constant; it does not promise a four-year tuition freeze. Dollar differences are arithmetic inferences from the stated rates and assumptions. Negative savings must remain negative when a large outside budget or a paid-term assumption makes the modeled route more expensive.

### How each university bills

- **Liberty residential:** $12,695 per semester for 12–18 credits, or $870 per credit for a smaller load. The default is 15 credits per semester: eight semesters without transfer, two with 30 credits remaining. This is four academic years versus one academic year of university work; outside study and approvals take additional time. A short final semester must use its part-time charge rather than incorrectly receiving the full-time rate.
- **Liberty online:** $410 per credit requires at least 12 credits in the billed semester. Smaller semester loads use $475 per credit. Eight-week subterms must not be mistaken for separate full-time-pricing semesters. At 15 credits per semester, the default comparison is eight versus two semesters.
- **SNHU:** $354 per credit. At two three-credit courses per eight-week term, 120 credits model 20 terms and 30 credits model five. The teaching-week count is a pace calculation, not a graduation date.
- **TESU:** The nonresident default uses 15 credits per 12-week billed term. Registration for 9–18 credits automatically uses $4,652 flat-rate tuition; smaller loads use $584 per credit. Multiplying every credit by the part-time rate would overstate this full-time baseline. NJ/PA resident prices differ.
- **Charter Oak:** $329 tuition per credit. The default assumes 15 credits per semester, with course availability and Cornerstone/Capstone sequencing separately checked. Required materials, services and technology fees are disclosed but excluded from the tuition comparison.
- **UMPI YourPace:** $1,800 per eight-week undergraduate paid session. The default eight-session/two-session illustration is consistent with 15 credits per session, but the calculator must use explicit paid-session assumptions. Transfer credits do not automatically reduce a flat session price or guarantee the number of sessions a student needs.
- **WGU BS Project Management:** $3,830 tuition plus $200 resources, or $4,030 per six-month paid term. Eight terms before transfer and three after are explicit pace assumptions, consistent with 120 CUs versus 32 remaining at roughly 15 CUs per paid term. They are not WGU's published program outline or a promised finish. Tuition changes only when the paid terms actually needed change.

## Transfer ceilings and source-origin limits

**Liberty:** The 75% ceiling is 90 credits in a 120-credit illustration. Degree and major/minor residency still apply. The current DegreePath residential course map proposes **83 outside credits** in its base draft; it is not an approved 90-credit map. The policy-limit cost illustration must not silently become a finished course plan. The 30-hour Experience Plus policy is not a verified universal cap on ACE courses.

**SNHU:** The bachelor's maximum is 90 credits. Eligible college credit and prior learning can combine, but each award still needs degree placement.

**TESU:** The combined ACE/NCCRS maximum is 90 bachelor's credits. A conditional six-credit Edison Accelerate finish implies a possible 114 outside credits, but requires eligible mixed source-origin credit, all remaining academic requirements and the current $3,468 Accelerate fee. This comparison uses the **standard route with 30 TESU credits**; it neither charges an Accelerate fee nor pretends 114 cheap ACE credits are available.

**Charter Oak:** The 114-credit ceiling applies to four-year institutional credit. Two-year institutional credit has an 87-credit limit. Non-collegiate course-provider credit has a separate 90-credit maximum and requires eligible agreements. Both the three-credit Cornerstone and three-credit Capstone must be completed at Charter Oak. The cost illustration uses 90 approved outside credits and 30 Charter Oak credits, not the absolute 114-credit institutional maximum.

**UMPI:** The university states no fixed incoming-credit limit. Ninety possible outside credits is arithmetic for a 120-credit degree with 30 required UMPI credits, not a published universal transfer cap. The 30 includes major and upper-level subsets; approved degree placement remains necessary.

**WGU Project Management:** The institutional ceiling is 75%, but the current 120-CU program pathway lists **32 CUs as nontransferable**. Therefore, **88 CUs is a theoretical upper bound**, not a verified 88-CU ACE course package. The cited community-college pathway itself grants 60 transfer CUs. Do not use 90/30 for this program. WGU's 110-CU BS Business Management program has different requirements and tuition and must not be substituted into this example. Incoming transfer must be evaluated before the initial WGU term begins.

## The outside-credit allowance

The default $2,000 is a planning input. It is not a quote from Sophia, Study.com, StraighterLine, CLEP or Saylor, and it does not identify a complete approved basket for any degree. Costs depend on the exact courses, recommendation versions, paid subscription windows, exams, labs, tax and other charges.

Current provider research supports an adjustable allowance rather than a universal low price:

- Sophia's live pricing page lists $99/month, $299/four months and $799/year. An older tutorial still displays $599 annually; use current live pricing and verify checkout.
- Study.com's current college-credit plans list College Saver at $95/month and Pro at $235/month. Current completion rules differ from older two-exams-per-month descriptions; source the active policy.
- StraighterLine has $99/month membership plus individual course prices, and separate new-student course bundles. Course prices, included bundle lists, labs and other exclusions must be checked.
- CLEP's current exam fee is $97, plus applicable test-center or remote-administration fees.
- Saylor Direct Credit lists a $5 designated proctored credit-exam session; an ordinary course-completion certificate is not automatically transferable credit.

An illustrative membership subtotal can be $1,518: four months of Sophia ($299), three months of Study.com Pro ($705), two months of StraighterLine membership ($198), and four StraighterLine courses assumed at $79 each ($316). That assumes a particular mixture of successful three-credit courses, timely completion and exact degree acceptance. It is not a mapped 90-credit degree plan or a timeline promise; actual costs can be higher. The public headline should use the editable allowance, not advertise this subtotal as a guaranteed package.

## Evidence and maintenance

Each dataset entry links the official tuition schedule, transfer/residency policy and relevant calendar or program source. The provider-source list links current public price and exam policies. Recheck dated prices and program rules during the monthly catalog review. New cost estimates must keep current-rate assumptions, financial-aid scope and unknown fees visible.

The comparison dataset does not change the private course-allocation engine, award earned credits, approve transfers, or replace the receiving school's evaluation. Its purpose is to help students understand the size and timing of a possible opportunity before building and confirming an individual plan.

## Running student estimate

### Remaining costs in Compare plans

Compare plans now prices the remaining course allocation for each valid scenario. It reuses the planner's provider pricing so a shared subscription or multi-requirement exam is not charged once per requirement. Completed university and outside coursework is excluded from future course charges. In-progress course charges remain included unless the student's updated assumptions account for payments already made.

The headline identifies its scope: tuition plus outside courses, a known subtotal when a component is missing, or a remaining cost estimate when the included fee assumptions are also complete. Unknown prices stay unknown. Provider sources, membership assumptions, paid university terms, missing charges and exclusions appear in the breakdown. Financial aid is not deducted, and housing, books and materials are excluded unless included in the student's fee estimate.

Cost and speed priorities affect the automatic shortlist. All eligible comparable routes need the relevant estimates before a lowest-cost or shortest-time winner is shown. A partial tuition-and-courses comparison can suggest a route with explicit exclusions; it does not establish the lowest all-in degree cost. Missing comparisons fall back to course fit. The same saved course collection is used for each route, and school-specific approvals do not follow a course into another degree.

### Savings indicator

The portal and worksheet use `savings-engine.js` to compare tuition from the student's **allocated course ledger**, rather than copying a school's policy maximum. It currently supports the researched Liberty residential business and TESU general-management course maps. Other targets show an estimate-pending state until the exact course and tuition models are available. Academic allocation is independently revalidated before any dollar estimate appears; a research gap remains explicitly provisional and is priced as university fallback, not described as a proven university-only requirement.

Planned outside placements, user-reported completions, and individually school-confirmed placements remain separate. Completing a course already included in the proposed plan increases progress without granting its projected savings again. Community outcomes do not award an individual transfer. Unknown-source or unallocated records never produce transfer savings. Flat tuition and partial final terms follow the same billing engine used by the public comparison; a three-credit change does not automatically reduce a paid term.

Outside-course budget and billed-term pace are explicit per-program student assumptions, applied only when the student chooses Apply. The optional detailed study schedule separately estimates completion weeks and prerequisite sequencing; billed terms avoided exclude outside study and processing time. A positive university per-credit quote explicitly overrides the published tuition assumption. Negative net differences are displayed as extra cost. Invalid saved assumptions offer correction without silently replacing private data. These settings stay in the existing private plan state and never enter the public community preview.

The default Liberty **actual course map** places 83 outside credits, leaving 37 at university under this incomplete map. At 15 credits per billed semester, published tuition changes from $101,560 to $31,480; adding the $2,000 outside-credit allowance produces a **$68,080 estimated difference** and five fewer billed semesters. This differs from the public 90-credit policy scenario. It is before financial aid and excludes other university fees, books, housing and living costs. It does not establish a guaranteed graduation date or an awarded saving.
