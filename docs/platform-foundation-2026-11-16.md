# Reviewed templates, research, and college access

## Student experience

Default program, school, comparison, and worksheet pickers offer 37 reviewed, usable templates. A complete requirement ledger alone does not qualify: a mandatory course choice with no selectable course keeps the template out of the default library. Published free electives remain visible choices. Existing private targets and older saved versions remain recoverable. Students can also paste one official degree link at `/request-plan.html` to reuse an existing route or research an editable private draft. School, award, format, and catalog are derived from observed official evidence instead of required upfront fields. Shared links with multiple known identities ask for the exact choice. The manual requirements builder remains available separately.

The curriculum registry includes all 45 researched models, including four standalone models that were previously missed. Six source-checked Liberty Criminal Justice aliases distinguish residential and online pages and map to their actual course ledgers. The reported residential URL resolves to 44 requirements and 120 credits; the current conservative transfer-first route uses 74 mapped outside credits and 46 Liberty credits. The 90-credit overall transfer ceiling is separate from the courses currently mapped. Source-plan responses are checked with the actual planner, and opening a plan re-fetches the catalog and model, rejects changed catalog/source versions, preserves the previous plan and courses, and goes directly to My plan. The public `/api/catalog/degree-link` lookup reads only preloaded sources; it never reads accounts or starts AI research.

Core workspace text, forms, touch targets, course actions, and the next-step button have been enlarged. Sign-in returns to the originating supported workspace, including a school or community discussion, without uploading a device plan. Marketing calls to action begin with available templates; school research resources distinguish available plans from research coverage.

Ten school insight profiles contain source-linked benefits, tradeoffs, tuition units, and accreditation facts. Two linked learner discussions are individual perspectives. There are no invented review counts or universal ease, speed, cost, or recognition scores. Comparisons keep degree levels, study formats, credit origins, and pricing units separate.

## Shared research and publication

### Coverage audit and documented course options

The version-43 baseline audit found 37 available requirement ledgers across four schools, but only four outside providers represented in modeled course options. Academic credit counts passed for all 37; this did not mean that every outside placement or elective choice was complete. Conditional candidates, missing mappings, and individual transcript evaluation had been mixed together. The baseline census and prioritized platform research backlog are retained in `content/reviews/coverage-audit-v43-2026-11-17.json` and `content/reviews/research-backlog-v43-2026-11-17.json`.

Reviewed public-template imports now preserve independently source-bound published evidence. The Liberty business example retains 105 documented options, including 63 receiving-school maps and 42 university courses, instead of downgrading everything to conditional. Seven weaker legacy matches still need research. Manual and private AI imports remain unreviewed, and no completed or university-confirmed student credit is created by importing a plan. Personal evaluation is represented separately from published course evidence.

The credit-source directory now contains 31 entries. New entries cover ALEKS, Westcott/UMass Global, Portage, UPI Study, SeeMore CSM, and Christian Leaders. Dated provider evidence includes recommendation periods, transcript issuers, separate learning/credit routes, pricing units, and actual eligibility. The research service receives the complete directory and 40 trusted public domains; these navigation inputs never become newly observed transfer evidence. Version-four AI provenance records the supplied directory, and changed coverage requires an explicit research refresh rather than an automatic paid call. A directory listing is not a completed degree mapping.

Five independently reviewed DSST exam-form awards now reach matching named Liberty online undergraduate requirements and their restricted elective alternatives. The records retain minimum score 400, separate three-semester-credit source/receiving quantities, current ACE versions, and separately dated Liberty guides. Receiving upper-level status follows Liberty's course, not the exam's source level. Residential and graduate inheritance is prohibited. The $100 exam fee is modeled per distinct exam, with variable test-center charges separate. A sixth English exam remains in the internal backlog because current provider documents disagree about forms.

Christian Leaders' current free-learning and eligible academic-transcript routes are represented separately. Its archived Liberty guide is not promoted to current equivalencies: the linked Homeschooling for College Credit article identifies that guide as outdated, and the current catalog differs on some quantities. The public calculator's documented JavaScript API was located, but current CLI records were not retrieved in this release. This remains DegreePath research work, not a task assigned to a student. The relevant audit is preserved in `content/reviews/christian-leaders-transfer-audit-2026-11-17.json`.

The next-step panel now directs documented courses to their course details, and missing public requirements to DegreePath's degree-link research. Published matches no longer generate a blanket purchase-confirmation warning. Actual conditional placements, student grades/scores, prior-record evaluation, residency, prerequisite, and academic-integrity checks remain in force. The degree-link result distinguishes unfinished platform research from personal record steps. This release does not claim that every listed provider or program has a fully researched route.

Supabase provides identity; Sites D1 stores private plans, community activity, research artifacts, and college permissions. Personal course records do not enter Claude research. Reviewer hints remain private request data. Canonical research identity includes the exact school, degree, award level, delivery format, catalog, and meaningful source URL parameters. Tracking parameters are removed. Ready curated plans and saved research are reused before another vendor call.

Claude is a bounded research assistant, not an approval authority. New artifacts are drafts. Publication requires university-scoped curriculum and policy sources, separately scoped receiving equivalencies and provider identities, explicit review, revision checks, and the same academic planner used by student plans. This checks real residency, upper-level, major, prerequisite, duplicate-course, source-origin, and credit-unit constraints. A reviewed template remains a proposed plan; it does not establish an individual student's admission or transfer decision.

Eight curated research-reference packets cover ODU, Troy, Regent, South College, UMass Global, Walden, Liberty, and Bellevue. Their 21 official sources support 13 bounded numeric facts plus conditional military, delivery-format, and pricing cautions. These are research inputs, not additional completed templates. Exact degree URLs and meaningful query parameters bind a packet; packet and supporting-source review dates must both be within 45 days. Uncertain catalog, credit-unit, and policy fields are scoped separately so an unresolved course-count discrepancy cannot erase a verified degree-total check. Missing source units are not inferred. An overall transfer ceiling never establishes blanket ACE acceptance, and subscription prices are not presented as whole-degree costs.

Research can read official HTML and PDFs through Claude's bounded web-fetch tool, with at most four fetches and 8,000 content tokens per fetched document in each existing provider call. New private quality-ready results require an actual successful university curriculum fetch; a search snippet, requested URL, or curated reference hint cannot satisfy that requirement. Reference conflicts feed the existing single refinement pass. Fetch failures do not become observed evidence, and valid fetched work can survive an unrelated search failure. Version-three provenance distinguishes these outputs from earlier cached drafts. No live paid Claude generation was performed for this release; provider protocol and planning behavior were exercised with controlled fixtures.

When one official page offers materially different routes, the app asks one source-backed question before starting research, such as course-based study versus Walden Tempo Learning. The trusted selection travels with its private request, progress, history, sign-in recovery, and cache identity, and remains on the imported personal model. The model cannot silently select a route, and one format's cached result cannot substitute for another. Expired known-choice sources block new research until refreshed instead of silently dropping that choice requirement. Private results are rechecked against the current reference bank before reuse and lose reusable status after 45 days without automatically spending on another call. The public degree-link endpoint may return reference metadata while still reporting that no usable plan is matched.

Private AI drafts can open directly in My plan without publication or a CSV detour. Only a source-bound, balanced requirement ledger can be imported; unsafe course options are removed, unknown policies stay unresolved, and legitimate unchosen electives remain visible gaps. The saved model records `ai-research` provenance with `reviewedByStudent:false` and never claims independently verified execution. Options, citations, policy evidence, sections, prerequisites, saved student courses, and earlier plan versions survive the import. The preview uses the same academic planner to respect residency and source caps; an infeasible draft shows available choices instead of claiming an optimized selection. Existing incomplete results can be rebuilt only through an explicit, owned, revision-checked action using the shared daily budgets. Raw model artifacts never serve as safe private course plans.

Public template lists are lightweight and paginated, with twelve cards by default and a maximum of twenty-four. Full artifacts are fetched only on opening and checked again before use. Personal copies preserve named transfer options, constraints, citations, prior versions, and existing student records. Reopening an existing copy does not replace student edits. Original source-credit quantities stay unknown where a receiving-school award is the only established fact.

Research runs have durable D1 claims, bounded output/search requests, a default global cap of ten calls per day, a maximum configurable cap of twenty, three calls per student per day, and twenty new distinct requests per student per day. Link-first research and explicit rebuilds allow up to 16,384 output tokens and six searches per provider call. A quality pipeline permits an initial pass and at most one targeted refinement, with each actual call charged separately against the existing run limits. It preserves the best usable result if refinement regresses. Server-owned versioned provenance and a native academic-planner check are required before a private AI result is treated as reusable. Incomplete cached drafts continue research only on an explicit Build action; saved partial metadata cannot override the trusted URL identity. A private progress endpoint exposes real stages and source/requirement counts through read-only polling. Interrupted leases can be resumed explicitly by their owner without automatic paid calls; public publication still requires administration. Cached results can be immediate; new research and inaccessible or complex source pages cannot promise immediate, complete results.

The live ODU test exposed a canceled research POST after about 33 seconds, before the first provider result. Negotiated research responses now stream an immediate acknowledgment and eight-second keep-alive comments, followed by the existing result or a safely encoded error. Authentication, ownership, CSRF, format selection, and budget checks run before research acceptance. Keep-alive comments establish connection activity; they do not claim academic progress. Provider waits default to three minutes and are capped at four minutes per call, below the five-minute lease; the connected stream has a 490-second hard deadline. A disconnected client cancels the current provider call, prevents an automatic repair, and retains saved findings. Short interruption cleanup may use `waitUntil`; the full research job does not. The app does not claim a distributed queue or guaranteed work after a browser closes.

The browser reads streamed results with bounded memory, preserves structured API error statuses, and never automatically retries a paid POST. A dropped connection checks owned status, continues read-only monitoring for a live lease, recovers a terminal saved result, or offers explicit Resume for an interrupted lease. If status cannot be reached, a Check saved progress action retains the degree link and selected format instead of displaying a raw browser network error. The repaired flow still requires a live signed-in generation test; the preceding failure is evidence for the transport fix, not a verified complete ODU plan.

The site owner connects an existing Claude Console workspace from `/admin.html`: create a workspace API key, paste it into **Claude connection**, and select **Save and test connection**. The fixed `/v1/messages/count_tokens` check validates access without generating paid research. A successful save enables new requests from `/request-plan.html`; existing queued requests can be started from the administration research queue. Drafts still require source and academic review before publication. Claude Console workspace spending limits provide a separate account-level control; application run caps are not dollar limits.

Managed credentials use AES-256-GCM encryption in D1 with a runtime-only `AI_CONNECTIONS_MASTER_KEY` secret. Only a server-validated owner can save, test, rotate, or disconnect a key. Neither responses nor audit records contain credentials. The browser clears key entry before submission and never persists it. Connection changes use revisions and a five-per-hour save/test quota; disconnect remains available after that quota. Managed credentials take precedence over the optional legacy `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` runtime secret, and removal explicitly reports any remaining environment fallback. A decryption failure cannot silently fall back to another credential. Optional configuration: `ANTHROPIC_MODEL`, `TEMPLATE_RESEARCH_TIMEOUT_MS`, `TEMPLATE_RESEARCH_DAILY_LIMIT`. Keys are resolved only for new research, after exact-template reuse and status checks. No live model invocation has been verified without an owner-provided key. Requests and manual planning remain available independently.

## Accounts and college access

Existing account controls use server-validated Supabase identities and secure HttpOnly cookies. Worker egress uses supported manual redirect handling and rejects redirects before credentials could be forwarded; browser fetches retain their own supported redirect mode. Private plan reads and writes are owner-scoped and use revision checks. Community member, moderator, and owner roles are server-controlled. College organization membership is separate from global moderation and cannot access private plans.

A college starts pending. Verification requires a recorded administrator review; owner-managed access grants require an existing account. Seeded university research is not evidence that a college team is authorized to receive leads. No organizations are automatically verified from the school directory.

Contact requests require an unchecked, explicit student permission for a named verified college. The server takes the verified account email, records consent text/version/time, and exposes only the selected contact fields to that organization's active authorized members. Private courses, transcripts, and full degree plans are excluded. Withdrawal blocks future access; revoked institutions and memberships cannot retrieve leads. This release adds no outgoing marketing messages, lead billing, or paid influence over academic rankings.

## Operations and scale limits

SQL changes are versioned in Drizzle migrations `0004_college_access`, `0005_reviewed_templates`, and `0006_ai_connections`, and `0007_research_progress`. The existing Supabase project was restored from paused status. Security advisors reported no findings. Email and Google sign-in are enabled; the owner confirmed Google login works. Update the Google authorized origins/redirect URI and Supabase site/redirect URLs when the official domain changes.

The foundations are implemented; capacity for millions of students has not been load-tested. Before that scale, measure authentication latency and quotas, D1 read/write load, backup/restore behavior, and end-to-end planning latency. Reduce write-on-read profile bootstrapping, normalize and independently cache large curriculum packs, and migrate personal template copies toward immutable shared version references. A single D1 database and current free-service limits require a measured partitioning or Postgres migration strategy as usage grows. Changing storage should preserve existing private-plan ownership and version semantics.

Source review remains a separate monthly operation. The existing pathway review queue covers pathways; the dated research-reference packets also require refresh and do not update themselves. AI or community popularity cannot silently change published academic facts. Browser rendering was not verified in this environment; executable academic, authorization, API, source, and transport checks are recorded in the release validation.
