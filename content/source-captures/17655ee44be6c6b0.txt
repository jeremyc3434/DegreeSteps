COURSE DESCRIPTION
Sophia Learning's U.S. History II course will build student knowledge of key events and figures in the history of
the United States from the post-Reconstruction era through 2015. This course emphasizes critical thinking skills
and consideration of multiple perspectives through the analysis of primary sources. Additionally, students will
learn how to think like a historian by utilizing primary and secondary sources to better understand historical
events and applying a historian’s lens to real-world and modern-day situations.
COURSE EFFECTIVE DATES: July 2026 - Present
PREREQUISITES: No prerequisites
LENGTH OF COURSE: This is a self-paced course. Students may use as much or as little time as needed to
complete the course.
ACE CREDIT® RECOMMENDATION: In the lower-division baccalaureate degree category, 3 semester hours in
U.S. History after 1877 (ACE ID: SOPH-0025).)
GRADING: This is a pass/fail course. Students must complete 17 Challenges (formative assessments), 5
Milestones (summative assessments), and 2 Touchstones (project-based or written assessments) with an overall
score of 70% or better.

Touchstones are projects that illustrate your comprehension of the course material, help you refine skills, and
demonstrate application of knowledge. Read further for information on the touchstones in this course:
Touchstone 2: Thinking Like a Historian (100 points): Students will apply skills of historical thinking by
tracing how one topic in U.S. history changed across a defined period of time. Students will explain what
changed, what stayed the same, and how broader historical forces shaped that evolution.
Touchstone 4: Historical Thinking with Primary Sources (100 points): Students will apply the Five Cs of
historical thinking (change over time, context, causality, contingency, and complexity) to a primary source
from recent U.S. history. Students will explain what this source reveals about its time, using it as a window
into a larger historical narrative.

LEARNING OUTCOMES
Upon completion of the course, the student will be able to:
. Recognize the causes and effects of urbanization, industrialization, and westward expansion in the late 19th
century and the tools and analytical lenses that historians use to research and interpret the past.
. Explain the political, social, and economic forces behind the emergence of the modern U.S. state and
contextualize historical events by tracing continuity and change over time.
. Analyze how World War II, the Cold War, and the civil rights revolution transformed the United States in the
mid-20th century by considering causation, context, and complexity.
. Interpret recent U.S. history by connecting past debates over government, economy, and culture to
contemporary domestic and foreign policy conflicts.
