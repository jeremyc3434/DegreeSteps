learn to inform, influence, and inspire.
The online Communication degree program at Bellevue University is designed to help students strengthen their ability to connect, influence, and lead through effective communication. Throughout the program, you’ll complete projects you can showcase to employers—including writing samples, research papers, annotated bibliographies, audience analyses, and public relations plans. These hands-on experiences allow you to build a professional portfolio that demonstrates your skills across multiple communication styles and media.
Apply what you know and earn credit for experience.
At Bellevue University, your previous academic work and real-world experience matter. Students can apply transfer credits, work experience, or military training toward their Communication degree—helping reduce the number of credits needed for completion.* The program values the knowledge you bring and integrates it into your studies, allowing you to connect classroom learning with professional and life experience in meaningful ways.
Develop skills for a wide range of careers.
This program emphasizes both the art and strategy of effective communication. You’ll gain experience crafting speeches, developing digital and traditional messaging, and applying principles of diversity and inclusion to communication planning. The program provides a strong foundation for careers in business, corporate communications, media, or law, and can also prepare you for graduate study in Business and Professional Communication or Pre-Law pathways.
what you'll learn.
In this program, you’ll learn to communicate with purpose and impact—applying communication theory and strategy to professional, academic, and real-world settings.
upon graduation, students will be able to:
Apply communication theories, perspectives, and concepts to a variety of communication contexts.
Create messages appropriate to audience, purpose, and context.
Explain the academic discipline of Communication and characterize the role communication plays in career development and civic engagement.

Communication Studies degree courses
Current students please login to BRUIN and select “Academic Progress” for your curriculum requirements.
Requirements (36 credit hours)
15 Credit Core:
CA 107 Public Speaking in the Digital Age (3 credits)
This course provides training in the fundamentals of public speaking in the 21st-century, mediated environment. By learning the principles of presentation development, organization, and delivery, students will be empowered to share their messages in a variety of forums.
CA 208 Critical Thinking and Communication (3 credits)
This course introduces argumentation as a model of critical thinking. Students will learn the foundation of critical thinking and how critical thinking positively impacts communication.
CA 309 Communication: Putting Theory into Practice (3 credits)
This course provides a wide range of communication theories with a special emphasis on direct application through the introduction of models/concepts designed to improve communication skills in a variety of contexts and communication environments. Competencies in the areas of relationship development, listening, socialization, cognitive processing, reducing uncertainty and dealing with conflict are addressed.
CA 412 Applying Critical Thinking for Successful Communication (3 credits)
Students will learn how to apply to their everyday lives the foundational information learned in CA 208. Arguments are analyzed and critiqued to develop skills in reasoning, using evidence, finding fallacious reasoning, and developing counterarguments and rebuttals.
Prerequisite: CA 208
CA 460 Communication Studies Capstone (3 credits)
This course requires students to reflect on and synthesize the knowledge and skills they have developed in Communication Studies courses. Emphasis is given to the nature and significance of Communication Studies as an academic discipline, the role of communication in civic life, and preparation for careers in communication. Prerequisite: Satisfactory completion of Communication Studies core (CA 107, CA 208, CA 309, CA 412) and senior standing.
In addition to the courses in the 15 Credit Core, select seven Communication Arts courses (21 credit hours) to complete the major requirements; 12 of these hours must be upper-level courses.
Additional CA courses may include the Corporate Communication and Effectively Communicating Yourself Course Clusters listed below.
CA 105 Introduction to Business and Professional Communication (3 credits)
This course introduces foundational communication skills for business and professional situations. Emphasis is placed on the skills of developing effective workplace relationships, collaborating with others in groups and teams, and presenting information to meet audience needs.
CA 308 Business Communication (3 credits)
Students will learn how to apply the foundational information learned in CA 105. This course provides theory and practice of written skills needed in business through models, concepts, and case studies relevant to the workplace, while addressing business ethics and diversity issues. Competencies in the areas of writing, organization of thought, creativity, and decision-making are addressed. Prerequisite: CA 105
CA 325 Organizational Communication (3 credits)
Communication is critical to the success of modern organizations. This course overviews key organizational communication approaches, theories, and concepts. Particular attention is given to ethically and effectively responding to organizational communication challenges and assessing one's own perspective as a communicator in the organizational context.
Complete Corporate Communication Cluster
Corporate communication professionals must have knowledge of and be able to manage an array of communication situations and media. In this course, students will learn the basics of how to create messages and manage various corporate communication situations.
CA 326 Introduction to Corporate Communication (3 credits)
This course is designed to provide a foundation for understanding professional communication and for developing basic corporate communication skills. This course orients the student to essential features of communication and develops competencies in conceptualizing oral and written communication and problem solving.
CA 400 Managing Internal Communication (3 credits)
This course introduces the practices and principles of communicating with internal stakeholders—specifically employees. The course emphasizes interactive employee communication programs, strategies and the manager's role in establishing an environment that encourages dialogue and the flow of information.
CA 426 Building Effective Organizations (3 credits)
This course focuses on training the competent communicator to train others in the organization. The course also emphasizes developing the skills necessary to design and develop interventions. Competencies include problem solving, coordinating and visioning.
Complete Effectively Communicating Yourself Cluster
These courses combine to create opportunities for students to directly apply course material on how to communicate effectively with individuals and in group situations. Emphasis is placed on sensitivity to non-verbal message exchange in formal and informal professional situations. A number of communication events that require direct questioning will be reviewed to help students increase their interviewing skills. Presentation requirements will be addressed to assist students to formally present themselves and their message to both small and large audiences. Throughout the cluster, students will be guided to improve their performance in any communication event by focusing on intrapersonal, interpersonal, and audience-involving situations
CA 303 Nonverbal Communication (3 credits)
This course addresses the application of nonverbal communication theories directed toward improved understanding of self and others in a variety of communication contexts. The course presents terminology, concepts, and models of nonverbal communication. Competencies in the areas of effective social interaction, intercultural awareness, conversational adaptability, self-awareness, and relational communication are addressed.

How long does it typically take to finish a degree?
The time it takes to finish a degree depends on factors such as transfer credits, course load, and whether you study full-time or part-time. Many adults pace their studies around work or family responsibilities, starting slowly and adjusting their load as they gain momentum. Students with previous credits may progress more quickly, while first-time learners may prefer a steadier pace. Estimating your timeline usually begins by reviewing past coursework, your weekly availability, and the pace that feels realistic for your situation.
Can I work full-time while earning a degree?
Working full-time while earning a degree is possible, and the best approach depends on your weekly schedule and course expectations. Many adults begin with one course to understand the workload before increasing their pace. Planning ahead, creating a weekly routine, and staying organized can help maintain steady progress. Flexible course structures often make it easier for working learners to balance school with professional and personal responsibilities.
How do transfer credits work when returning to finish a degree?
Transfer credits are evaluated by comparing your previous coursework to current program requirements to see where past classes may still apply. An evaluation helps returning learners understand which credits remain usable and what requirements still need to be met. This clarity supports adults in planning their remaining academic pathway.
What is online learning like compared to taking classes on campus?
Online learning is a flexible format where you complete coursework through weekly modules instead of attending scheduled class meetings. Most courses include readings, assignments, recorded lectures, and discussion activities that keep learners engaged. Because participation is asynchronous, you can move through material at times that fit your schedule. This structure appeals to adults balancing school with work or family commitments while still offering consistent instructor guidance.
How do I know which degree program is the best fit for my goals?
You can determine which degree fits your goals by reflecting on your interests, long-term plans, and the types of work you enjoy. Reviewing course descriptions and learning outcomes can highlight how programs differ. Many adults also consider past experiences or strengths that feel energizing or meaningful. Speaking with advisors or professionals may offer additional insight. A good-fit program usually feels both engaging and aligned with the future you envision.
Will earning a degree help me stay competitive in the job market?
A degree may help you stay competitive by strengthening transferable skills such as communication, analysis, and problem-solving. Coursework often introduces concepts relevant across many industries and helps learners understand organizational expectations. While advancement depends on several factors, continued learning can help adults stay adaptable as workplace needs and technologies evolve.
What kinds of skills do most degree programs help students develop?
Most degree programs help students develop analytical thinking, communication, and problem-solving skills. Coursework teaches learners to interpret information, evaluate options, and apply concepts in practical situations. Many programs also emphasize collaboration, digital literacy, and effective written communication. These broad competencies support adaptability and help graduates contribute in a variety of professional environments.
What support is available for students returning to school after a long break?
Students returning after a long break can access support such as tutoring, writing assistance, academic advising, and time-management tools. Many adults begin with one course to rebuild study habits and reestablish confidence. These resources help learners ease back into academic expectations while balancing school with work and family responsibilities.
Are online degrees respected by employers?
Online degrees are generally respected by employers when they come from accredited institutions and are backed by strong coursework and demonstrated skills. Most employers focus on a candidate’s abilities, experience, and readiness to contribute rather than the format in which the degree was completed. Accreditation, curriculum quality, and faculty expertise typically matter more than delivery method.
How can a degree add value beyond career opportunities?
A degree can add personal value by strengthening confidence, broadening perspective, and supporting long-term goals. Many adults find that coursework provides structure, momentum, and opportunities to master new concepts. Completing assignments and meeting academic expectations can reinforce resilience and a sense of progress. Education often contributes to personal development in ways that extend beyond career advancement.
