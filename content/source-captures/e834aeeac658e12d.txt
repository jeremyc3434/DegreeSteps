plan for real-world business solutions with a degree in project management.
Gain the skills employers look for by learning how to plan, manage, and deliver projects from start to finish. In this Bachelor of Science in Project Management program, you’ll apply what you learn to a real-world problem through a hands-on capstone project—so you graduate with experience, not just theory.
You’ll be trained to follow industry-recognized project management standards at every stage of the project lifecycle. That focus on quality is why our program is accredited by the PMI Global Accreditation Center (GAC), a distinction held by only a small number of project management programs worldwide. Coursework in this program helps satisfy the required PMI educational hours.
Position yourself for growth with a project management degree.
This bachelor’s in project management is built to align with the Project Management Institute (PMI) framework and industry best practices, giving you the knowledge and real‑world skills needed to pursue PMI certifications—especially the Project Management Professional (PMP)®—by preparing you to:
Confidently lead projects from initial concept to final delivery, ensuring scope, schedule, cost, and quality stay in balance.
Drive project performance with strong resource planning, budgeting, scheduling, and risk‑management strategies.
Elevate your communication and leadership capabilities through impactful stakeholder engagement, collaborative teamwork, and sound decision‑making across diverse environments.
The courses in this degree may be applied towards PMI’s 35 hours of project management education/training required to sit for the PMP® exam. They may also support ongoing professional development and recertification for PMI credentials such as PMP®, CAPM®, PMI-ACP®, and others—based on your career goals and PMI requirements.
Learn more about eligibility and requirements here: https://www.pmi.org/-/media/pmi/documents/public/pdf/certifications/overview-guide-and-brochure.pdf

Project Management degree courses

Requirements (36 credit hours)
PMGT 305 Fundamentals of Project Management (4 credits)
This course introduces fundamental project management concepts, processes, and knowledge areas and illustrates the guiding principles that drive the development and management of projects. Focus is placed on identifying strategic direction and meeting stakeholder expectations by developing processes and strategies to achieve business goals and objectives. This course emphasizes ethical decision-making and critical thinking in the project environment. Prerequisite: Enrollment in the Project Management accelerated degree completion program.
PMGT 315 Project Leadership and Communications (4 credits)
This course focuses on project management, leadership, and communication. Students learn how effective leaders use communication to align teams, drive performance, and manage change. The curriculum covers major leadership styles—traditional, relationship-based, servant, value-based, and transformational—and their impact on teams and organizations. Key topics include team engagement, avoiding management of pitfalls, leading diverse teams, and mastering project communication through active listening, emotional intelligence, and stakeholder-focused messaging. The course also addresses best practices for meetings, conflict resolution, and building strong relationships with executive sponsors and stakeholders through clear communication plans. Prerequisite: Enrollment in the Project Management accelerated degree completion program.
PMGT 335 Project Cost (4 credits)
This course addresses techniques for developing and controlling costs within a project while utilizing various estimating tools. Coverage includes cost estimates, baselines, and controls in terms of validating actions, reporting, cost outcomes, and variances. Earned Value Management techniques will be introduced and used to interpret the status of a project at any point in the lifecycle. Prerequisite: Enrollment in the Project Management accelerated degree completion program.
PMGT 340 Project Quality (4 credits)
This course addresses the processes and procedures involved in assuring that projects satisfy the objectives. This course emphasizes managing project quality by identifying and applying relevant quality management tools, activities, and methods. Topics include quality planning, quality assurance, quality control, and process improvement. Prerequisite: Enrollment in the Project Management accelerated degree completion program.
PMGT 400 Project Procurement Management (4 credits)
This course addresses how to determine the items to be purchased or acquired in order to meet the project goals, when to acquire the items, and the options for procurement. Topics include vendor selection, contracting and negotiation. Prerequisite: Enrollment in the Project Management accelerated degree completion program.
PMGT 410 Project Risk Management (4 credits)
This course will provide an in-depth analysis of risk management methodologies, from both the strategic and tactical perspectives. State-of-the-art tools and techniques for identifying, measuring, and monitoring risks in the project management environment are examined. Prerequisite: Enrollment in the Project Management accelerated degree completion program.
PMGT 425 Applied Project Management (4 credits)
This course is the first of two project planning courses where students apply project management knowledge and skills acquired in previous courses to address an actual business need/project. This course focuses on project selection, initiation and planning phases, to include selecting, defining, and preparing a project and plan. By the end of the course, students will have developed their project's charter, baselines, scope, and comprehensive project management plan. Prerequisite: Enrollment in the Project Management accelerated degree completion program.
PMGT 427 Information Technology Project Management (4 credits)
This course introduces students to the fundamentals of project management within the context of Information Technology (IT). Grounded in the Project Management Institute’s Project Management Body of Knowledge (PMBOK®) standards, principles, and domains, the curriculum covers both traditional and Agile methodologies, with a special emphasis on Scrum practices. Advanced Project Management is the second and final course dedicated to the team capstone project. The focus of this course is establishing approaches for executing, monitoring, controlling, and closing their capstone project. By the end of the course, each student will have completed each process phase of the project and will deliver a final student presentation to fellow students in the cohort. Prerequisite: Enrollment in the Project Management accelerated degree completion program.

How long does it typically take to finish a degree?
The time it takes to finish a degree depends on factors such as transfer credits, course load, and whether you study full-time or part-time. Many adults pace their studies around work or family responsibilities, starting slowly and adjusting their load as they gain momentum. Students with previous credits may progress more quickly, while first-time learners may prefer a steadier pace. Estimating your timeline usually begins by reviewing past coursework, your weekly availability, and the pace that feels realistic for your situation.
Can I work full-time while earning a degree?
Working full-time while earning a degree is possible, and the best approach depends on your weekly schedule and course expectations. Many adults begin with one course to understand the workload before increasing their pace. Planning ahead, creating a weekly routine, and staying organized can help maintain steady progress. Flexible course structures often make it easier for working learners to balance school with professional and personal responsibilities.
How do transfer credits work when returning to finish a degree?
Transfer credits are evaluated by comparing your previous coursework to current program requirements to see where past classes may still apply. An evaluation helps returning learners understand which credits remain usable and what requirements still need to be met. This clarity supports adults in planning their remaining academic pathway.
What is online learning like compared to taking classes on campus?
Online learning is a flexible format where you complete coursework through weekly modules instead of attending scheduled class meetings. Most courses include readings, assignments, recorded lectures, and discussion activities that keep learners engaged. Because participation is asynchronous, you can move through material at times that fit your schedule. This structure appeals to adults balancing school with work or family commitments while still offering consistent instructor guidance.
How do I know which degree program is the best fit for my goals?
You can determine which degree fits your goals by reflecting on your interests, long-term plans, and the types of work you enjoy. Reviewing course descriptions and learning outcomes can highlight how programs differ. Many adults also consider past experiences or strengths that feel energizing or meaningful. Speaking with advisors or professionals may offer additional insight. A good-fit program usually feels both engaging and aligned with the future you envision.
Will earning a degree help me stay competitive in the job market?
A degree may help you stay competitive by strengthening transferable skills such as communication, analysis, and problem-solving. Coursework often introduces concepts relevant across many industries and helps learners understand organizational expectations. While advancement depends on several factors, continued learning can help adults stay adaptable as workplace needs and technologies evolve.
What kinds of skills do most degree programs help students develop?
Most degree programs help students develop analytical thinking, communication, and problem-solving skills. Coursework teaches learners to interpret information, evaluate options, and apply concepts in practical situations. Many programs also emphasize collaboration, digital literacy, and effective written communication. These broad competencies support adaptability and help graduates contribute in a variety of professional environments.
What support is available for students returning to school after a long break?
Students returning after a long break can access support such as tutoring, writing assistance, academic advising, and time-management tools. Many adults begin with one course to rebuild study habits and reestablish confidence. These resources help learners ease back into academic expectations while balancing school with work and family responsibilities.
Are online degrees respected by employers?
Online degrees are generally respected by employers when they come from accredited institutions and are backed by strong coursework and demonstrated skills. Most employers focus on a candidate’s abilities, experience, and readiness to contribute rather than the format in which the degree was completed. Accreditation, curriculum quality, and faculty expertise typically matter more than delivery method.
How can a degree add value beyond career opportunities?
A degree can add personal value by strengthening confidence, broadening perspective, and supporting long-term goals. Many adults find that coursework provides structure, momentum, and opportunities to master new concepts. Completing assignments and meeting academic expectations can reinforce resilience and a sense of progress. Education often contributes to personal development in ways that extend beyond career advancement.
