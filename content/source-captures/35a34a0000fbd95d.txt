Part One: Program Overview
The University of Maine at Presque Isle is submitting a program proposal for the development of
a 90-credit B.A. Applied Psychology degree program. This degree, to be offered in our
competency-based education modality- YourPace program- aligns with the university's mission
to provide accessible, high-quality education that prepares students for professional success, as
well as the University of Maine System9s mission of improving the quality of life for people in
Maine and around the world. Additionally, with fewer required credits, tuition costs are
significantly reduced, making higher education more accessible and affordable for students,
especially working professionals, non-traditional learners, and degree completers.
The Applied Psychology program aims to address the growing need for mental health
professionals and provide students with an accelerated, flexible, cost-effective pathway to a
degree that meets workforce demands. The 90-credit degree option provides employers
evidence of discipline-specific knowledge and foundational competence, especially when paired
with relevant internships or research experience. This initiative will fortify opportunities for
degree completion and will address the unique needs of adult learners and career changers,
while aligning with strategic objectives at both the institutional and system-wide levels.
Specific Goals/ Objectives:
Enhance Workforce Readiness: Equip students with applied psychological knowledge and
practical skills to meet the growing demand for mental health professionals, particularly in rural
and underserved areas.
Promote Accessible and Flexible Education: Provide a competency-based, fully online learning
experience through the YourPace program, catering to working professionals, non-traditional
learners, and degree completers.

Support Career Advancement: Prepare graduates for entry-level positions in psychology-related
fields or further graduate study, emphasizing areas such as mental health counseling,
behavioral health, and industrial-organizational psychology.
Ensure Academic Excellence: Maintain academic rigor and quality through experienced faculty
instruction, established curriculum, and comprehensive student support services.
Contribute to Regional and National Workforce Needs: Align program outcomes with both state
and national workforce priorities by addressing critical shortages in mental health services and
promoting career mobility within psychology-related professions.
Part Two: Evidence of Need
The demand for mental health professionals continues to rise, particularly in rural and
underserved regions where access to psychological services remains limited. The University of
Maine at Presque Isle (UMPI) seeks to address this critical workforce gap by offering a
streamlined, 90-credit, Applied Psychology degree. This program is designed to provide an
accelerated and flexible educational pathway that enables students to enter the workforce more
efficiently while maintaining affordability and academic rigor.
Recent labor market analyses underscore the growing need for professionals in psychology-
related fields. According to Hanover Research, programs in Psychology are above average in
growth (historical completions and projected employment). Psychology programs typically
represent strong opportunities for program development or expansion of existing programs.
Based on data from Lightcast, job postings for psychology-related roles are projected to
increase by 3.6% between 2023 and 2025, with an estimated 736,798 positions posted
annually. Additionally, the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) projects that employment in
psychology-related fields will experience significant growth over the next decade, particularly in
areas such as mental health counseling, behavioral health, and industrial-organizational
psychology. These sectors are expected to expand by more than 14%, a rate that substantially
exceeds the national average for all occupations. The increasing demand for psychological
expertise is driven by heightened awareness of mental health issues, expanded healthcare
access, and evolving workplace dynamics that necessitate behavioral and cognitive expertise.
In addition to job availability, salary trends highlight the economic viability of careers in
psychology. As of May 2022, the median annual wage for psychologists was approximately
$85,000, while mental health counselors earned a median salary of $49,000. These figures
indicate both the financial sustainability of careers in psychology and the long-term professional
opportunities available to graduates of the proposed program.
While other UMS institutions offer Bachelor's degrees in Psychology- including the University of
Maine; University of Maine Machias (Bachelor of Arts in Psychology & Community Studies):
University of Southern Maine and University of Maine Farmington- the University of Maine at
Presque Isle is the only institution to offer competency-based education through the YourPace
program. This program offers an additional degree option that creates a flexible pathway to a
Bachelor9s or Master9s degree, with several graduate programs in Psychology and Counseling

offered in the University of Maine System at the University of Maine Farmington, University of
Southern Maine, and University of Maine.
While current competing (non-UMS) B.A. in Applied Psychology programs in New England are
unclear due to the nascent nature of the 90-credit model, it is noteworthy that Merrimack
College (Massachusetts), Plymouth State University (New Hampshire), and Johnson & Wales
University (Rhode Island) have all received approval to offer Applied Bachelor's (90-credit
degree) programs.

Part Four: Program Content
Proposed Catalog Description
The B.A. in Applied Psychology at the University of Maine at Presque Isle is a 90-credit degree
program designed to provide students with a foundational understanding of psychological
principles and their practical application across a variety of professional settings. The program
emphasizes the development of critical thinking, communication, problem-solving, and data
analysis skills essential for careers in mental health services, education, business, and human
resources.
Through a combination of academic coursework, experiential learning, and opportunities for
research and internships, students gain practical experience and preparation for graduate study
or entry into the workforce. The streamlined curriculum allows for flexibility and is well-suited for
transfer students and working adults seeking a focused path to degree completion. Graduates
will be equipped to apply psychological knowledge in diverse, real-world contexts and to make
meaningful contributions in their chosen fields.

Psychology Core/ General Concentration Course Descriptions
Course Title: PSY 100 | General Psychology | 3 Credits
Course Description: Provides a general introduction to psychology, the scientific study of
behavior and mental processes. The course presents major theoretical perspectives,
methodologies, and key findings in physiological, cognitive, motivation, learning, developmental,
personality, abnormal, and social psychology.
Prerequisite(s): A passing score on both the reading and writing placement tests or successful
completion of ENG 100 or a non-degree seeking student
Course Title: PSY 205 | Lifespan Development | 3 Credits
Course Description: Surveys changes in cognitive, personal, and social functioning from before
birth through old age. Using a life-span framework, emphasizes the principles, theories, and
methods that psychologists use.
Prerequisite(s): PSY 100
Course Title: PSY 311/SOC 311 | Research Methods | | 3 Credits
Course Description: Presents fundamentals of research design, measurement, and data
analysis. Applications of research methods within the behavioral sciences, including scientific
writing and professional ethics.
Prerequisite(s): SOC 100 or PSY 100, and MAT 101 or MAT 201

Course Title: MAT 101 | Basic Statistics | 3 Credits
Course Description: Data collection through sampling and experimentation, ethics of sampling
experimentation, bias and precision, graphical and numerical summaries of data, confidence
statements, association and causation between two variables, probability.
Prerequisite(s): College-level math placement or math practicum as co-requisite
Course Title: MAT 201 | Probability and Statistics | | 3 Credits
Course Description: The goal of this course is to present the basic ideas of probability theory
and statistics to students in various fields. Upon completion of this course, students will have an
understanding of producing, organizing, and analyzing data and making inferences. Students
may take this course as a one-semester statistics course or continue to take MAT 202 as a two-
semester sequence.
Course Title: MAT 202 - Probability and Statistics Il | 3 Credits
Course Description: Linear regression, correlation, multiple regression, chi-square tests,
experimental design, analysis of variance, non-parametric statistics.
Prerequisite(s): MAT 201
Course Title: PSY 200/SOC 200 | Social Psychology | 3 Credits
Course Description: This course is cross listed as SOC 200
Considers the processes by which individuals interact with others, influence and are influenced
by others, and are shaped by society. Includes topics such as the self, attitudes, prejudice,
stereotypes, interpersonal attraction, group dynamics, and persuasion.
Prerequisite(s): PSY 100 or SOC 100
Course Title: PSY 235 | Abnormal Psychology | 3 Credits
Course Description: Examines the classification, determinants, and indications of mental illness
and its treatments. Topics include schizophrenia, personality disorders, mood disorders,
anxiety-related disorders and substance-related disorders.
Prerequisite(s): PSY 100

Course Title: PSY 312 | Research Methods II | 3 Credits
Course Description: Presents intermediate issues in research design, hypothesis-testing, data
analysis, and technical writing in psychology. Promotes understanding of relevant empirical
literature, preparation of spreadsheets and graphic display of data, and applications of statistical
analyses. Class research projects offer foundation for independent work.
Prerequisite(s): PSY 311/SOC 311
Course Title: PSY 341 | Drugs and Behavior | 3 Credits
Course Description: Surveys information of recreational and therapeutic drugs and their effect at
various levels including that of the nervous system, individual behavior, and broader society
consequences.
Prerequisite(s): PSY 100 or SOC 100
Course Title: PSY 355 | Group Process | 3 Credits
Course Description: Provides theoretical examination of group dynamics, group behavior, and
individual behavior within groups. The course considers norms, roles, status, power,
communication, productivity, motivation, member diversity, leadership, and conflict resolution.
The class also offers experiential practice as a small group, and explores one9s own emotions,
motivations, and skills in group participation.
Prerequisite(s): PSY 100 or SOC 100
Course Title: PSY 360 | Animal Behavior | 3 Credits
Course Description: Explores similarities and differences in behavior among a variety of species
and considers the implications of such differences. Major topics will include behavioral genetics,
evolutionary theory, natural selection, mate choice and reproduction, communication, sociality,
and foraging.
Prerequisite(s): PSY 100 or BIO 112

Course Title: PSY 370 | Personality | 3 Credits
Course Description: Presents principles and issues in scientific study of personality. Emphasis
on description and assessment, classical theories, controversial issues, and recent research.
Prerequisite(s): PSY 100 and PSY 311/SOC 311
Course Title: PSY 375 | Sex and Gender | 3 Credits
Course Description: Investigation of gender in North American society. Feminist, social
constructionist, sociobiological, and social psychological perspectives will be explored. Focus is
on the creation of gender identities, inequalities, cultural expectations, and cultural
representations of gender. Other topics include childhood socialization, sexualities, gender
activities, and social power.
Prerequisite(s): PSY 100 or SOC 100
Course Title: PSY 380 | Principles of Counseling | 3 Credits
Course Description: Introduces theories and techniques of psychological counseling. Considers
the counseling relationship and the personal and professional issues involved in becoming a
counselor. Emphasis on determinants and evaluation of counseling success.
Prerequisite(s): PSY 235 and PSY 370
Course Title: PSY 374 | Vocational Aspects of Disability | 3 Credits
Course Description: This course explores the many aspects of assisting clients with various
disabilities as they transition from adolescence, or are rehabilitated as adults, to the adult world
of work. Working with individuals who may have mental illness or with cognitive, physical, or
developmental disabilities or substance abuse issues is explored. Emphasis is placed on
assessing and working with their strengths, needs, interests, and aptitudes. The supports and
barriers of society, government and private agencies, and individuals will be examined, with an
overarching goal of helping clients to reach their full potential, while maintaining their personal
dignity.
Prerequisite(s): SOC 100 or PSY 100

Course Title: PSY 382 | Substance Abuse Counseling | 3 Credits
Course Description: This course explores the definition, causes, theories, and treatment
approaches to the process of addiction, with emphasis upon dual diagnosis clients. Working
with mentally ill and substance misusing clients in various settings is explored. Emphasis is on
application of recent research to the philosophy and methods of treatment.
Prerequisite(s): PSY 100 and PSY 235, or permission of the instructor
Course Title: PSY 350 | Special Topics in Psychology | 3 Credits
Course Description: Explores special topical areas within psychology for further explanation of
topics beyond that possible in other existing courses. Students in collaboration with an instructor
may fashion course to meet needs and interests of those involved. Course may be repeated
twice if topic is different or at an advanced level from that previously taken by individual
students.
Prerequisite(s): PSY 100
Course Title: PSY 404 | Psychology of Learning | 3 Credits
Course Description: Basic principles of learning will be examined with an emphasis on their
application to understanding and modifying behavior. Theories, models, research, and
applications of learning principles to education, child-rearing, counseling, and biofeedback.
Laboratory simulation approaches will demonstrate concepts developed with animals, but
readily applicable to children and adults.
Prerequisite(s): PSY 100
Course Title: PSY 446 | Psychobiology | 3 Credits
Course Description: Explores the relationship between behavior and activity of the nervous,
sensory, and endocrine systems. Topics include behavior genetics, learning and memory,
emotions, sleep, and behavioral disorders.
Prerequisite(s): PSY 100 or BIO 112

Course Title: PSY 448 - Cognitive Psychology | 3 Credits
Course Description: Surveys research and theory on human cognition. Laboratory exercises will
include attention, memory, problem solving, language, and creativity and other topics. Format
includes lectures and laboratory activity.
Prerequisite(s): PSY 100 and PSY 311/SOC 311 Recommended: PS
Course Title: PSY 475 | History & Systems of Psychology | 3 Credits
Course Description: An examination of the theoretical and historical framework underlying
psychology as a field and a profession. Emphasis on clinical psychology, school psychology,
industrial- organizational psychology, counseling psychology, and their links to training of
professionals. Integrates principles from the major content areas and promotes professional
research and writing in APA style.
Prerequisite(s): Senior Standing, PSY 311/Soc311 and two 300/400 level courses in psychology
Course Title: PSY 480 | Advanced Research 1 | 3 Credits
Course Description: Student designs and conducts empirical research under supervision of
psychology faculty member. Course may be repeated for a total not to exceed six credits
Prerequisite(s): PSY 312, junior or senior standing, and consent of instructor
Course Title: PSY 496 | Practicum 1
Course Description: Supervised field experience in a social service or other setting where
psychology background and skills are especially useful. Course may be repeated for a total not
to exceed six credits
Prerequisite(s): Psychology major or minor with junior or senior standing and consent of
instructor

Advising methods:
Students in UMPI9s YourPace program are advised by a team of New Student Enrollment
Specialists and Academic Success Coaches. New Student Enrollment Specialists assist
students in selecting and enrolling in courses that align with their degree requirements and
personal objectives. Academic Success Coaches maintain regular contact with students,
helping them create personalized learning plans, navigate the learning management system,
prepare for assessments, and connect with campus resources. Their proactive engagement is
instrumental in promoting student retention and success.
Accommodations for diverse accessibility needs under the Americans with Disabilities Act
(ADA):
This fully online, asynchronous academic program is designed to meet all ADA and Section 504
guidelines by ensuring accessibility in every aspect of course delivery. All digital content,
platforms, and materials will comply with WCAG 2.1 standards, featuring screen reader
compatibility, captioned videos, transcripts, and accessible assessments. The program will
support assistive technologies and offer reasonable accommodations, including extended time
and alternative formats, through a proactive partnership with UMPI9s Student Support Services/
Disability Services Office. Faculty will be trained in accessible content creation and inclusive
teaching, and student feedback will ensure continuous improvement. Admissions,
communication, and support services will also be fully accessible, ensuring equitable
participation for all students.

Faculty Resources
The University of Maine at Presque Isle (UMPI) currently has a dedicated and experienced team
of faculty well-versed in online instruction and competency-based education (CBE). Faculty
members are equipped to deliver high-quality instruction aligned with the program's learning
outcomes and CBE methodology. No additional faculty hires are required at this time, as the
existing team has the capacity to support the anticipated enrollment and program structure.
Staff Resources
UMPI has a strong support system in place, including academic advisors, student success
coaches, and administrative personnel who are experienced in managing and supporting online,
competency-based programs. These staff members are integrated into UMPI9s existing CBE
infrastructure and will continue to provide personalized guidance, technical assistance, and
administrative support. No additional staffing resources are needed at this time.
Library Resources
UMPI9s library offers extensive digital collections that support the needs of online learners,
including access to academic journals, eBooks, research databases, and subject-specific
resources. The current library holdings and services are fully sufficient to support this new
program. Students also benefit from remote access to librarian support and research
assistance, ensuring comprehensive academic support without the need for physical resources.
Equipment
The university has all necessary digital infrastructure in place to support the delivery of a fully
online, competency-based program. This includes use of D2L9s Brightspace, a robust learning
management system (LMS), video conferencing tools, and access to technical support services.
All instructional and administrative technologies required to deliver and manage the program are
currently in use and meet or exceed standards for online education. No new equipment is
needed.
Facilities
As a 100% online competency-based program, this initiative does not require dedicated physical
facilities. All instruction, advising, and support services are delivered remotely, allowing students
to progress through the program from any location. UMPI9s existing virtual infrastructure
ensures that all educational needs are met without reliance on campus-based facilities.
