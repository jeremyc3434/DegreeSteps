a bachelor of public health, tailored to the career you want.
Learn the key skills and knowledge to develop educational initiatives that promote better health within communities. While working on your online public health degree, you can choose from several courses that align with your personal career goals. You can promote healthy living for students in grade levels 7-12 by becoming a health educator. Earn a BS in Education degree with a Health Education subject endorsement. Check out the requirements for teaching endorsements here.
what you'll learn.
In this program, you’ll learn to design, implement, and evaluate health education initiatives that promote wellness, advance health equity, and strengthen communities.

Analyze community health needs, assets, resources, and capacities.
Design comprehensive health education and health promotion programs for diverse populations.
Evaluate health education and health promotion programs.
Communicate health information professionally in a variety of contexts.

Public Health degree courses
Current students please login to BRUIN and select “Academic Progress” for your curriculum requirements.
Requirements (50 credit hours)
Core (41 credit hours)
BI 201 Anatomy and Physiology I (4 credits)
This course introduces basic concepts, anatomical terminology, cell structure and function and histology, followed by an in-depth study of the anatomy and physiology of the following organ systems of the human body: Integumentary, arthrology, muscular, cardiovascular and lymphatic systems. A mandatory laboratory component supports and amplifies the lecture material and allows the student to study microscopic anatomy on slides and to perform dissection on representative animal models. An online component allows the student to practice course content with additional exercises. Prerequisites: BI 100 required for Biology and Kinesiology majors, strongly recommended for Health Science majors.
BI 202 Anatomy and Physiology II (4 credits)
This course consists of an in-depth study of the gross and microscopic anatomy and the physiology of the following organ systems of the human body: The central and peripheral nervous, endocrine, sensory, respiratory, digestive, reproductive, and excretory systems. A mandatory laboratory component will support and amplify the lecture material and allow the student to perform dissection on representative animal models. An online component will allow the student to practice course content with additional exercises. Prerequisites: BI 201 or Instructor permission (3 credit hrs lecture; 1 credit hr lab)
BI 206 Nutrition Through the Life Span (3 credits)
This course consists of the study of the nutrients required by the human body, the foods from which they are obtained, their utilization by the body and their importance for the maintenance of optimal health throughout the life span.
CA 315 Health Communication (3 credits)
This course examines the theory and practice of health communication. Emphasis is placed on interpersonal relationships, social and cultural issues, and mediated messages concerning health communication.
HHS 260 Gender and Culture (3 credits)
This course offers an analysis of research, practice, and policies concerning gender, culture and health. Focus will include the examination of the intersections of social categories, such as gender, race / ethnicity, social class, and sexuality, with conceptualizations of health, healthcare delivery, and health-related policies. Students will apply theory to the understanding, critique, and construction of health, explore health- related issues within a global context, evaluate ethical dilemmas in real-world case studies, and critically analyze the impact of power in specific health-related areas.
PHE 200 Introduction to Public Health Education (3 credits)
Public health is a growing discipline that strives to promote health of the community. This course provides an introduction to the history, science, and principles of public health, as well as opportunities to learn about current issues and approaches in the field.
PHE 210 Understanding Health Research (3 credits)
The focus of this course is on the consumption of healthcare research and the application of evidence-based practice for healthcare professionals. This course introduces types of research methodologies, data collection, analysis of data, and the interpretation and application of best practices in healthcare settings.
PHE 230 Foundations of Health Behavior (3 credits)
This course provides an introduction to health behavior as a key construct of the public health role. This course will cover topics in health behavior, culture, communication, as well as perspectives applicable to individuals, groups, and communities. Focus will be to explore emerging trends and theories of health behavior and provide a platform to investigate evidence based interventions.
PHE 310 Public and Consumer Health Literacy (3 credits)
This course examines relevant, contemporary issues related to health literacy and the role health literacy has on an individual and the public's ability to obtain, process and understand basic health information and services needed to make appropriate health decisions.
PHE 330 Epidemiology (3 credits)
This course is essential for public health educators in a variety of settings. This course focuses on the study of the distribution and determinants of disease, health conditions or events among a population with emphasis on current health problems and issues as it relates to real life application to address public health programs and health policy.
PHE 360 Personal and Community Health (3 credits)
This course is designed to develop the understanding, attitudes, and practices that contribute to better individual and community health.
PH 410 Medical Ethics and Leadership (3 credits)
This course examines relevant definitions and topics of medical ethics and the process of decision making within medical environments.
PY 211 Human Development (3 credits)
This course will present the principles of human growth and development from conception to death. Topics to be included in the course are methods of child study, consideration of individual differences, and analysis of patterns and sequences of development.
Choose 3 courses (9 credit hours) from the following list:
PHE 410 Foundations of School Health (3 credits)
This course examines the history and role of health education in today's society. Covers the philosophical principles and models utilized in the delivery of health education.
PHE 415 Public Health Disaster Management (3 credits)
This course examines management of public health emergencies and disasters at the local, state, federal level in both the private and public sectors.
PHE 420 Program Planning for Community Health (3 credits)
This course provides students with an in-depth overview of strategies, techniques, and approaches necessary in public health to plan programs, to assist communities in improving health status, and recognize health disparities, all while considering determinants of health.
PHE 430 School Health Education and Planning (3 credits)
This course provides students with the theoretical knowledge and skills to design a comprehensive school health education plan through use of collaborative strategies to benefit student learning, families and key stakeholders.
PHE 455 Health Education Program Evaluation (3 credits)
This course will provide students with essential knowledge and skills to understand and apply evaluative methods to health education programs. Specifically, students will gain understanding of effective frameworks to improve existing programs, plan health education strategies, and demonstrate results of resource investment.
PHE 480 Health Education Program Administration (3 credits)
This course provides students with knowledge and skills necessary to effectively manage health education programs. Specifically, students will examine leadership, decision making, fiscal, and physical resources necessary to execute health education programming.

How long does it typically take to finish a degree?
The time it takes to finish a degree depends on factors such as transfer credits, course load, and whether you study full-time or part-time. Many adults pace their studies around work or family responsibilities, starting slowly and adjusting their load as they gain momentum. Students with previous credits may progress more quickly, while first-time learners may prefer a steadier pace. Estimating your timeline usually begins by reviewing past coursework, your weekly availability, and the pace that feels realistic for your situation.
Can I work full-time while earning a degree?
Working full-time while earning a degree is possible, and the best approach depends on your weekly schedule and course expectations. Many adults begin with one course to understand the workload before increasing their pace. Planning ahead, creating a weekly routine, and staying organized can help maintain steady progress. Flexible course structures often make it easier for working learners to balance school with professional and personal responsibilities.
How do transfer credits work when returning to finish a degree?
Transfer credits are evaluated by comparing your previous coursework to current program requirements to see where past classes may still apply. An evaluation helps returning learners understand which credits remain usable and what requirements still need to be met. This clarity supports adults in planning their remaining academic pathway.
What is online learning like compared to taking classes on campus?
Online learning is a flexible format where you complete coursework through weekly modules instead of attending scheduled class meetings. Most courses include readings, assignments, recorded lectures, and discussion activities that keep learners engaged. Because participation is asynchronous, you can move through material at times that fit your schedule. This structure appeals to adults balancing school with work or family commitments while still offering consistent instructor guidance.
How do I know which degree program is the best fit for my goals?
You can determine which degree fits your goals by reflecting on your interests, long-term plans, and the types of work you enjoy. Reviewing course descriptions and learning outcomes can highlight how programs differ. Many adults also consider past experiences or strengths that feel energizing or meaningful. Speaking with advisors or professionals may offer additional insight. A good-fit program usually feels both engaging and aligned with the future you envision.
Will earning a degree help me stay competitive in the job market?
A degree may help you stay competitive by strengthening transferable skills such as communication, analysis, and problem-solving. Coursework often introduces concepts relevant across many industries and helps learners understand organizational expectations. While advancement depends on several factors, continued learning can help adults stay adaptable as workplace needs and technologies evolve.
What kinds of skills do most degree programs help students develop?
Most degree programs help students develop analytical thinking, communication, and problem-solving skills. Coursework teaches learners to interpret information, evaluate options, and apply concepts in practical situations. Many programs also emphasize collaboration, digital literacy, and effective written communication. These broad competencies support adaptability and help graduates contribute in a variety of professional environments.
What support is available for students returning to school after a long break?
Students returning after a long break can access support such as tutoring, writing assistance, academic advising, and time-management tools. Many adults begin with one course to rebuild study habits and reestablish confidence. These resources help learners ease back into academic expectations while balancing school with work and family responsibilities.
Are online degrees respected by employers?
Online degrees are generally respected by employers when they come from accredited institutions and are backed by strong coursework and demonstrated skills. Most employers focus on a candidate’s abilities, experience, and readiness to contribute rather than the format in which the degree was completed. Accreditation, curriculum quality, and faculty expertise typically matter more than delivery method.
How can a degree add value beyond career opportunities?
A degree can add personal value by strengthening confidence, broadening perspective, and supporting long-term goals. Many adults find that coursework provides structure, momentum, and opportunities to master new concepts. Completing assignments and meeting academic expectations can reinforce resilience and a sense of progress. Education often contributes to personal development in ways that extend beyond career advancement.
