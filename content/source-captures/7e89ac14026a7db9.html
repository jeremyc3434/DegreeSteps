1. About UPI Study

UPI Study Inc. is an online course provider. We develop and deliver self-paced, college-level courses that carry credit recommendations, so that students can complete foundational and elective coursework independently and present it for transfer credit at a degree-granting institution.

We have served more than 130,000 students across a catalog of 88 courses with ACE and/or NCCRS credit recommendations, spanning business and accounting, computer science, mathematics, English, the sciences, health, psychology and the social sciences, history, humanities and the arts, and world languages.

UPI Study builds its own technology. Our learning platform, assessment engine, proctoring layer, and science laboratory simulators are developed in house, which allows us to enforce academic integrity at the level of the assessment itself rather than at the level of policy alone. Section 5 describes that infrastructure; Section 6 describes what it enforces.

2. Purpose and scope

Because our courses are asynchronous and completed without a fixed class meeting schedule, we hold ourselves to a higher evidentiary standard than a transcript line. Every graded component of every course is supervised, logged, and reproducible on request.

This document sets out how course outcomes are established, how attainment is assessed, what controls verify that the enrolled student is the person doing the work, what happens procedurally when a violation is suspected, and what documentation UPI Study provides to a receiving institution. It applies to every enrolled student and every graded assessment, without variation by location, enrollment date, or partner channel.

3. Course design, faculty review, and external evaluation

3.1 Syllabus of record and learning outcomes

Every UPI Study course has a syllabus of record. It is the controlling document for that course and states the course title and course code, the credit-hour value, prerequisites, required and supporting instructional materials with full citations, the unit-by-unit topic sequence, the measurable course learning outcomes, the grading structure, and the academic integrity standard in force. Outcomes are written as observable, assessable statements aligned to the cognitive levels appropriate to the course level.

Outcomes are benchmarked against the same course as commonly taught at accredited institutions, so that a UPI Study course covers a scope and depth comparable to a resident section. Where a national disciplinary standards framework exists, outcomes are mapped to it.

3.2 Faculty authorship and pre-release review

Courses are authored and reviewed by subject-matter faculty holding qualifications appropriate to the discipline. No course is released to students until college faculty have reviewed it. A master's degree in the discipline or a closely related field is the minimum; many UPI Study faculty hold doctorates, many hold or have held teaching appointments at accredited colleges and universities, and most bring five or more years of professional experience in the field they teach. Curricula vitae and degree documentation for the instructor of record are available on request.

3.3 External credit-recommendation review

UPI Study, Inc. has been a member organization of the National College Credit Recommendation Service (NCCRS) since January 2024, and is listed in the ACE National Guide. Course categories currently listed with NCCRS include Business and Accounting, Computer Science, English, French, Healthcare Management, Math, Psychology and Sociology, and Science.

These are the same evaluating bodies that establish credit recommendations for CLEP and DSST examinations and for military training. Under both frameworks, external faculty evaluators examine the syllabus, instructional materials, assessment instruments, grading practice, credit-hour substantiation, and integrity controls before a recommendation is issued.

Re-evaluation is scheduled, not one-off. NCCRS courses are re-reviewed on a four-and-a-half to five year cycle; ACE courses are re-reviewed every three years. Between external reviews, UPI Study conducts its own annual review of course content, assessment design, and integrity practice.

Current credit recommendations, by course, with recommended semester hours and the degree category to which they apply, are listed in the NCCRS CCRS Online directory under UPI Study, Inc. and in the ACE National Guide. Each UPI Study transcript carries the specific recommendation for each course and directs the receiving institution to the NCCRS directory to verify it independently.

3.4 Instructor access and student support

Self-paced does not mean unsupported. Each course has a named instructor of record, and students may raise subject-matter questions on the course material, their assignments, and their results. Questions are submitted through the platform and answered within 24 hours. Administrative, technical, and accommodation enquiries are handled by the UPI Study team at team@upistudy.com.

3.5 Currency and revision control

Courses are revised when instructional materials change edition, when outcomes drift from disciplinary norms, or when assessment data indicate an outcome is not being reliably measured. Every substantive revision is versioned, dated, and retained, so the syllabus in force at the time a given student completed the course can be reproduced exactly. Each syllabus of record carries its own version date on the document.

4. Course structure and assessment

4.1 The graded components

Every UPI Study course is assessed across four components. Three are directly supervised; the fourth is fully logged. The weighting applied to each is stated on that course's syllabus of record and follows the structure under which that course was evaluated:

  • Courses carrying an NCCRS credit recommendation weight the four components equally — documented engagement, quizzes, assignments and the proctored final examination at 25 percent each.
  • Courses that additionally carry an ACE credit recommendation follow the structure reviewed under that evaluation — quizzes 20 percent, assignments 30 percent, and the proctored final examination 50 percent.
  • Laboratory courses weight graded laboratory reports at 70 percent and quizzes at 30 percent, reflecting an assessment model in which the practical work is the primary evidence of attainment. They have no final examination and no minimum attendance score.

In every course except the laboratory courses, the documented-engagement requirement applies: a student must reach an attendance score of at least 70 percent of the instructional hours stated for that course in order to complete it.

4.3 Failure and retake

A student who does not pass the proctored final examination gets one free retake of the examination. A student who does not pass the retake must restart the entire course from the beginning. Prior progress, prior quiz scores, and prior assignment grades are not carried forward.

Laboratory courses have no final examination; they are graded as their syllabus states, on lab reports (70%) and quizzes (30%).

A course may be restarted once. A student who fails the restarted course is suspended from re-registering in that course for 30 days.

This is deliberately stricter than an open re-sit policy. A student gets one retake, not repeated attempts, so familiarity with the examination cannot be accumulated across attempts, and a failed retake cannot be converted into a pass by repetition alone — the whole course must be completed again on its own merits.

4.4 Documented engagement and credit-hour substantiation

UPI Study records the time each student spends in each lesson and each assessment. This serves the credit-hour substantiation requirement that both ACE and NCCRS apply: it is direct evidence that a student engaged with the material for a duration consistent with the semester hours recommended, rather than an assertion that they did.

Each course is built to the credit-hour standard applied by the evaluating body: the total student workload — instruction, practice, assignments, laboratory work, and assessment — is designed to be consistent with the semester hours recommended for that course. Each syllabus of record states that course's total instructional hours, and a student must reach a documented attendance score of at least 70 percent of those hours to complete the course. Laboratory courses are the exception: they are graded on lab reports (70%) and quizzes (30%) only.

Transparency to the receiving institution. Self-paced study allows a well-prepared student to move faster than a resident section. Rather than obscure that, UPI Study reports the start date and completion date of every course on the official transcript, so the receiving institution can apply its own judgment on pacing rather than relying on ours. Per-lesson and per-session engagement logs for an individual student are available on request, subject to a signed student release.

4.5 Assignments and projects

Assignments are substantial pieces of work, not short-answer exercises. Each is governed by a published rubric and is designed to require five to ten hours or more of sustained effort for a final assignment or project. Students submit not only the finished work but the proof of work behind it — drafts, working notes, data, sources, and the reasoning at each step.

The design matters more than the supervision here. Assignments require the student to set out and explain their reasoning step by step, applied to the specific case in front of them. Work of that shape cannot be lifted intact from a source or a generative tool without the substitution becoming visible in the reasoning itself.

Verification. A student is then assessed on their own assignment under supervised conditions, to confirm that they can account for the work they submitted. A student who cannot explain their own submission has not demonstrated the outcome, regardless of the quality of the artifact.

4.6 Discipline-specific assessment

Where an outcome cannot be validly measured by a written selected-response instrument, the assessment changes rather than the outcome.

  • English and writing courses include supervised timed writing components, so that composition is assessed under conditions where the writing is demonstrably the student's own.
  • French and world language courses include listening and speaking laboratory components alongside written work, so that aural comprehension and oral production are assessed directly rather than inferred.
  • Science courses include laboratory work carried out in UPI Study's own in-house laboratory simulators, built specifically for these courses and instrumented for proctoring in the same way as our examinations. Students perform the procedure, record observations, and are assessed on their results and interpretation.

4.7 Assessment designed to resist substitution

The most effective defence against substituted work is not surveillance — it is an item a substitute cannot answer. UPI Study therefore builds assessments around performance rather than recall, at every level of the course including quizzes.

  • Quizzes are practical, not recall-based. Students are asked to carry out a procedure and report what happened, not to select a remembered fact. An item asks what a specific simulation produced under specific conditions, or what a given data set yields when the procedure is applied to it.
  • Steps are assessed, not only answers. In quantitative and technical work, students enter the intermediate steps of their solution, not a final value alone. A correct endpoint reached without the working does not earn the mark. This defeats the most common failure mode in online assessment, in which a student obtains an answer elsewhere and enters it.
  • Laboratory items are tied to the student's own session. Science items reference the results the student produced in their own logged simulator session. Because those results vary by session, an answer copied from another student, an outside source, or a generative tool does not match the student's own recorded data and is immediately visible as inconsistent.
  • Simulation-specific conditions. Because UPI Study builds its own simulators, item conditions are set per session. There is no published external answer set for a UPI Study laboratory item, and no stable question-and-answer pair for a generative tool to have been trained on.

The effect is cumulative. A student attempting to outsource an assessment must reproduce a procedure they did not perform, on data generated in a session they did not run, and set out the intermediate steps that produced it — while monitored, on a clock calibrated to independent work, and with every navigation away from the assessment recorded against them.

4.8 Outcome-to-assessment mapping

Every course outcome is mapped to at least one supervised summative assessment, and the final examination is blueprinted so that each outcome carries a defined share of the examination. This mapping is maintained per course and is the artifact that answers the question, "how do you know the student learned what you say they learned?" A sample map appears at Appendix C; full maps for any course are provided on request.

4.9 The independent-pass requirement

A student must independently pass the proctored final examination in order to pass the course, regardless of the grades earned on other components. A student who does not meet the minimum score on the proctored final, including its one free retake (Section 4.3), does not receive credit for the course and cannot compensate for that result with other work.

Laboratory courses have no final examination; they are graded as their syllabus states, on lab reports (70%) and quizzes (30%).

This is the load-bearing control. In every course with a final examination, no combination of unsupervised work can carry a student to credit: the examination is the gate, and the examination is supervised.

5. Learning environment and technology

5.1 Platform

UPI Study delivers courses through a combination of a hosted commercial learning platform and UPI Study's own learning and assessment platform, developed in house to provide integrity controls beyond what commercial LMS products offer. Together they hold the enrollment record, all course content, quiz delivery and scoring, assignment submission, engagement logging, proctoring, laboratory simulation, and the gradebook of record.

Each student holds an individual account tied to a verified email address. Enrollment, progression, submission timestamps, per-item response times, quiz attempts and scores, proctoring records, laboratory sessions, and course completion are all recorded against that account and retained as the system of record.

5.2 Proctoring infrastructure

Examination and quiz supervision runs on a commercial proctoring platform together with UPI Study's own proctoring layer, which we developed to extend the detection set described at Section 6.3. Both enforce the same published standard; students face identical requirements regardless of which layer surfaces an event.

5.3 In-house laboratory simulators

Science laboratory work runs on simulators UPI Study built for that purpose. Because they are ours, they are instrumented in the same way as our examinations: sessions are logged, activity is monitored, and the student's procedure and results are recorded rather than self-reported. A student cannot submit laboratory results without a corresponding logged session in which they were produced.

5.4 Whether the student works entirely within the platform

Almost entirely. Instruction, quizzes, laboratory work, examinations, and grades are delivered, completed, and recorded inside the student's UPI Study environment, under logged sessions.

The one exception is assignments, which are take-home by design. A student writes an assignment away from the platform, as they would at any institution, and then submits it — along with the proof of work behind it — into the platform, where it is screened, graded, and recorded. As set out at Section 4.5, the student is subsequently assessed on their own submission under supervised conditions.

No graded work is transacted by email, by telephone, or off-record. A student requires no account, licence, or software beyond their UPI Study credentials and a standard modern web browser with a working webcam — including for laboratory work, which runs in the browser on our own simulators rather than through a third-party vendor.

5.5 Accessibility and accommodations

Students requiring an accommodation — extended examination time, alternative examination arrangements, or an alternative to camera-based monitoring — may request one at team@upistudy.com. Approved accommodations are documented and applied without penalty to the grade or to the integrity standing of the attempt. Where camera-based monitoring is not workable, an alternative supervised arrangement is provided; supervision is never simply waived.

6. Academic integrity framework

UPI Study treats academic integrity as an engineering problem as well as a policy problem. A prohibition that is not enforced by the design of the assessment is not a control. Every graded component therefore carries active enforcement.

6.1 Enrollment verification

Each student holds an individual account created at enrollment and tied to a verified email address. Before a proctored assessment opens, the student's enrollment record is confirmed, so that every attempt is tied to the registered account and appears against that record. All progress, submissions, assessment attempts and results are recorded against that account and retained as the system of record.

6.2 How assessments are supervised

Quizzes and examinations run in a monitored session. Leaving the assessment — opening another tab, another window, or another application — is detected and recorded, and carries a penalty applied to the course grade. Students are told this before they begin, and the penalty is applied consistently rather than at discretion. Assessment content may not be captured, reproduced, or shared, and doing so is a violation under Section 7.1.

Final examinations add AI proctoring: continuous session monitoring, idle detection, and per-item response timing recorded to the second. Idle detection is the control that matters most against a second device — a period of inactivity inconsistent with working the item, indicating attention directed off-screen, is detected and recorded, so a student consulting an outside source is not invisible simply because the source is not on the monitored machine.

Escalation. A flagged event pauses the attempt and issues a formal warning that the student must acknowledge. A further flagged event terminates the attempt, and the student must restart the course rather than simply re-sit the assessment. Students are shown this standard before they begin. There is no hidden threshold and no undisclosed penalty.

Human judgment. Flags raised on a final examination are reviewed by a person. Automated detection surfaces an incident; a person makes the determination. No adverse determination is ever made by an automated system alone.

Evidence. Each flagged event is recorded with a timestamp and the control triggered. Every attempt produces a session record with a unique reference code, retained under Section 7.6 and available to a receiving institution on request.

6.4 Assignments

Assignments are take-home and are secured by design, by evidence, and by verification rather than by live supervision:

  1. Constructed to resist substitution. Rubric-governed work requiring explained, step-by-step reasoning on a specific case, at a scale of five to ten hours or more.
  2. Proof of work required. Students submit drafts, working notes, sources, and intermediate reasoning alongside the finished artifact.
  3. Automated originality and AI screening. Every submission is screened by UPI Study's own originality and AI-detection tooling, developed in house alongside our proctoring platform.
  4. Human review. Every flagged submission is reviewed by a qualified reviewer, who examines the underlying matches and the reasoning before any determination. No penalty is applied on the basis of a similarity or AI-detection score alone.
  5. Supervised verification. The student is assessed on their own submission under proctored conditions and must be able to account for the work.

Two-strike escalation. A first sustained finding requires the student to redo the assignment. A second sustained finding requires the student to restart the course. The schedule is published to students in advance and applied consistently.

6.5 Assessment design safeguards

  • Randomised item draw. Each assessment form is drawn from a larger validated item bank, so students do not sit identical papers and a circulated answer key has limited value.
  • Timing calibrated to independent work. Limits are set so a student working from their own knowledge finishes comfortably, while a student consulting an outside source for each item cannot — and, under activity monitoring, the attempt to do so is detected.
  • Item types resistant to lookup and to generative tools. Figure-dependent items, multi-part scenarios that carry a result forward, interpretation of a supplied data set, and items requiring the student to identify why a given answer is wrong.
  • Cross-checking of supervised against unsupervised performance. A material gap between a student's assignment performance and their proctored examination performance is surfaced for review.
  • Item bank maintenance. Items are rotated on a scheduled cycle, and items showing anomalous performance are retired.

6.6 Use of generative AI

UPI Study's position is that generative AI may be used to learn, but never to produce. A student may use an AI tool to have a concept explained to them. A student may not use one to solve an assigned problem, to generate the worked steps, or to write any part of a submission. The distinction is published to students in those terms, and it is enforced rather than asserted.

ContextPosition and how it is enforced

QuizzesProhibited. Enforced by monitored sessions with navigation detection and a grade penalty, and by per-item timing.
Assignments and projectsPermitted only as a study aid — to help the student learn or understand a concept. UPI Study does not treat an automated AI-detection score as evidence of misconduct in itself. Such scores carry false positives at rates unsuitable for a disciplinary determination, particularly for students writing in English as an additional language. A flag opens a review; a qualified human reviewer, working from the proof of work, the reasoning in the submission, and the student's supervised performance, makes the determination.

6.7 Handling of proctoring data

Students are told in plain language what is monitored, and consent before each proctored assessment opens. Proctoring data is collected for academic integrity purposes only. It is not used for any other purpose, is not sold, and is not disclosed to third parties except to a receiving institution on a signed student release or where required by law.

Session records for attempts producing no referral are retained for 12 months and then purged. Where an attempt is referred, the record is retained with the case file under Section 7.6. A student may request confirmation of what was recorded for their own attempt.

7. Handling suspected violations

7.1 What constitutes a violation

  • having another person complete any part of a course or assessment, or completing one on another student's behalf;
  • using unauthorised resources, notes, devices, people, or AI tools during a proctored assessment;
  • submitting work that is not the student's own, including plagiarised, purchased, or AI-generated content presented as original;
  • submitting laboratory results not produced in a logged session by the student;
  • capturing, reproducing, or distributing assessment content;
  • falsifying identity, records, or accommodation documentation;
  • circumventing or attempting to circumvent proctoring or platform controls.

7.2 Detection and referral

A matter may be referred from four sources: an automated proctoring flag; an originality or AI screening review; a reviewer observation, including a performance gap between supervised and unsupervised work or an inability to account for a submitted assignment; or a third-party report. A referral is a question, not a finding.

7.3 Review and determination

  1. The referral is logged with a case reference and all relevant evidence is preserved.
  2. The student is notified in writing within 14 business days, told specifically what was flagged and what evidence exists, and invited to respond in writing within 10 business days of that notice.
  3. The UPI Study academic team reviews the evidence together with the student's response and makes a determination on the balance of the evidence.
  4. The determination, the reasoning, and any sanction are recorded in writing and provided to the student.

Grades are held, not voided, while a review is open. A flagged attempt that survives review carries no adverse record.

7.4 Sanctions

Sanctions are published in advance and applied consistently. See Appendix B.

7.5 Appeals

A student may appeal in writing within 15 business days of notification. The appeal is heard by a member of the UPI Study academic team who was not involved in the original determination, who reviews the full record and issues a written decision within 20 business days. That decision is final within UPI Study.

7.6 Records, retention, and disclosure

Integrity case records — referral, evidence, student response, determination, and appeal outcome — are retained for five years from the date of determination. Proctoring session records for attempts that produce no referral are retained for 12 months and then purged.

UPI Study maintains a case log of all referrals and determinations. Incidence figures for a given period are provided to a partner institution on request.

UPI Study will disclose the integrity standing of a completed course to a receiving institution on receipt of a signed student release, or as otherwise permitted by applicable law. A course completed with a sustained integrity finding is not certified as complete and is not transcripted as such.

Appendix A — Student academic integrity statement

The following appears in every course syllabus and is presented for acknowledgment before each proctored assessment.

Your work must be your own. UPI Study courses carry college credit recommendations. That recommendation means something only if the person who earned it is the person who did the work.

In practice: you complete laboratory work and examinations alone, without notes, other people, other devices, or AI assistants; quizzes are open book, so you may use your notes and course materials, but you still complete them alone, without other people or AI assistants; you submit written work that you wrote and reasoned through yourself; you disclose AI use where a course permits it; and you do not copy, photograph, or share assessment content.

Your work is recorded against your own account. Your enrollment record is confirmed before each proctored assessment, so every attempt is tied to you.

Your quizzes and examinations are monitored. If you leave the assessment — another tab, another window, another application — it is detected, it is recorded, and it costs you marks. Your final examination is AI-proctored, and the time you spend on each question is recorded. You will be shown exactly what is monitored and asked to consent before you begin.

If something is flagged, you receive one warning. The attempt pauses, you are told what was flagged, and you acknowledge it before continuing. If anything is flagged again, the attempt ends and you must restart the course.

Assignments carry two chances. You will be asked to submit your working, not only your finished piece, and you will be assessed on your own assignment afterwards. If a submission is found not to be your own work, you redo it. A second finding means restarting the course.

Review is not a verdict. A flag is checked by a person, not decided by software. You will be told what was flagged and what the evidence is, and you will have the chance to respond in writing before any decision is made. You may appeal.

If you need an accommodation, including an alternative to camera-based monitoring, contact team@upistudy.com before you sit the assessment. Requesting an accommodation never counts against you.

3Terminated proctored attempt sustained on review, or a second sustained assignment finding.The student must restart the course from the beginning. No credit is awarded for the affected attempt, and prior progress is not carried forward.
4Impersonation, purchased or contracted work, falsified identity or documentation, or a repeat Level 3 finding.Failure of the course, revocation of any credit recommendation already certified for it, and permanent withdrawal of enrollment eligibility. Disclosed to a receiving institution on request or on student release.

Every sanction is recorded in writing with its reasoning, provided to the student, and retained under Section 7.6.
