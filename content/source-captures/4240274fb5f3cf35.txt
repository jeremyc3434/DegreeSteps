E086 - Computer Systems for AI - This course examines computer systems and architectures through the lens of AI engineering, emphasizing how
hardware and software decisions shape the feasibility, performance, cost, and reliability of modern AI workloads. Learners analyze the roles of CPUs,
GPUs, TPUs, memory hierarchies, storage, interconnects, and distributed systems in enabling efficient training and inference. The course emphasizes
architectural tradeoffs, profiling and bottleneck diagnosis, and resource management strategies that influence latency, throughput, scalability, and
operational constraints. Through applied analysis and system design activities, learners translate functional and non-functional requirements into
concrete compute, memory, bandwidth, and power constraints across cloud, embedded, and edge environments. Learners evaluate failure modes and
safe degradation behaviors and apply infrastructure foundations, including standardized runtime environments and repeatable configuration concepts,
to support defensible deployment decisions. By the end of the course, learners can design and justify system architectures aligned to real-world AI
production constraints and organizational objectives. pipelines, apply word embeddings, and use sequence-based and transformer-based models to

E087 - Deep Learning for AI Engineers - This course provides a foundational orientation to the field of AI engineering, focusing on the essential
interplay between programming, mathematics, and real-world applications. Learners apply core programming principles and foundational mathematical
ideas that support algorithmic thinking and analyze how these foundations enable the design and development of AI systems. Students also describe
how AI engineering differs from related domains, such as data science and academic research, with an emphasis on applied engineering practice.
Through conceptual frameworks, applied examples, and introductory analysis, students begin to understand the skills, mindset, and responsibilities of
AI engineers, including considerations related to scalability, reliability, and responsible use of AI technologies. The course establishes a strong
foundation for success in the AI Engineering program by clarifying expectations for rigor and by highlighting the relevance of foundational
competencies for building trustworthy, impactful, and real-world AI solutions.
E088 - Natural Language Processing for AI Engineers - This course develops students’ ability to apply natural language processing (NLP) techniques
to solve engineering problems involving human language data. Learners implement reproducible text preprocessing and tokenization pipelines, apply
word embeddings, and use sequence-based and transformer-based models to complete tasks such as classification, sentiment analysis, and question
answering. The course emphasizes rigorous evaluation beyond accuracy, using task-appropriate metrics, structured error analysis, and stress testing
under realistic input variation, to identify failure modes and reliability risks. Learners also assess bias, safety, and responsible-use concerns (including
generative AI risks when applicable) and produce governance artifacts that document intended use, limitations, evaluation results, and monitoring
expectations. Through applied engineering work, learners demonstrate judgment in selecting methods and designing solutions that meet technical,
ethical, and operational constraints.
E089 - Computer Vision for AI Engineers - This course prepares learners to design, implement, and deploy computer vision solutions in engineering
contexts. Learners identify techniques for data selection, processing, structure, and generation for training computer vision models; apply CNN-based
models for detection, classification, and segmentation; and recommend appropriate tools and deployment strategies for vision models given a
business problem. The course builds on machine learning and deep learning foundations, narrowing the focus to vision-specific tasks with hands-on
model development, optimization, and deployment.
E090 - Applied AI Engineering - This capstone course requires students to integrate and apply engineering knowledge from mathematics,
programming, data engineering, machine learning, deep learning, and software engineering to design, validate, and deploy an AI‑enabled system that
addresses a real‑world problem. Learners work through the full AI system lifecycle, including problem formulation, model development, testing,
deployment, and monitoring. The course emphasizes ethical and responsible AI practices, systematic evaluation using appropriate metrics, and
production deployment using industry‑standard MLOps tools such as cloud infrastructure, containerization, CI/CD pipelines, and monitoring
frameworks. Through this culminating experience, learners demonstrate the ability to design and deploy AI solutions that meet technical, ethical, and
operational constraints expected of entry‑level AI engineers.
E091 - Building A Successful IT Organization - Building a Successful IT Organization addresses what makes a successful IT division by considering
the functions, roles, and responsibilities of IT professionals and specialists across various organizational environments and situations. Students will
also learn to develop a strategic plan, including goal setting, implementation planning, and understanding IT organizational structure.
E092 - Nurse Educator Foundations - Nurse Educator Foundations introduces the multidimensional roles of the contemporary nurse educator across
academic and practice-based settings. The course emphasizes the educator’s work as a leader, change agent, and curriculum innovator while
examining professional identity formation and ethical and legal foundations for practice. The course also addresses academic integrity, diversity,
equity, inclusion, and social determinants of learning, along with strategies for personal and professional growth such as self-care, career planning,
and engagement with professional organizations. Prerequisites include all prior courses in this programmatic sequence.
E093 - Innovative Teaching Strategies for Nursing Education - Innovative Teaching Strategies for Nursing Education prepares nurse educators to
design and implement innovative and experiential teaching strategies across didactic, clinical, simulation, and virtual learning environments. The
course emphasizes inclusive instructional design that supports professional identity development, critical thinking, and clinical judgment for diverse
learners. The course also addresses psychologically safe and accessible learning environments and the use of simulation and other instructional tools
to create authentic learning experiences. Prerequisites include all prior courses in this programmatic sequence.
E094 - Curriculum Design in Nursing Education - Curriculum Design in Nursing Education focuses on the principles and processes of developing
learner-centered curricula aligned with accreditation expectations and the needs of academic and practice-based settings. The course emphasizes
curriculum analysis, alignment of program and course outcomes, and practical approaches to course and program design. The course also addresses
interprofessional expectations and equity-informed curriculum decisions that account for social determinants of learning in diverse educational
contexts. Prerequisites include all prior courses in this programmatic sequence.
E095 - Assessment and Evaluation Strategies in Nursing Education - Assessment and Evaluation Strategies in Nursing Education equips nurse
educators to design, implement, and evaluate fair and meaningful assessments across academic and professional development settings. The course
emphasizes assessment quality, fairness, and alignment with professional practice expectations, including approaches such as test blueprints, rubrics,
and performance-based evaluation. The course also supports the use of assessment evidence and feedback to guide continuous improvement and
strengthen educational effectiveness. Prerequisites include all prior courses in this programmatic sequence.
E096 - Promoting Change and Innovation in Nursing Education - Promoting Change and Innovation in Nursing Education prepares nurse educators to
identify emerging issues that shape nursing education and to lead improvement initiatives. The course emphasizes leadership, scholarship, and
advocacy that advance health equity and respond to evolving professional and societal priorities. The course culminates in a formal nursing education-
based improvement project proposal in an academic or practice setting that is implemented and evaluated across Nursing Education Practicum I and
Nursing Education Practicum II. Prerequisites include all prior courses in this programmatic sequence.
