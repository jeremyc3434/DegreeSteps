U.S. History after 1877

Objective:
The course objective is to build student knowledge of key events and figures in the history of the United States from the post-Reconstruction era through the early 2020s. This course emphasizes critical thinking skills and consideration of multiple perspectives through the analysis of primary sources. Additionally, students learn how to think like a historian by utilizing primary and secondary sources to better understand historical events and applying a historian’s lens to real-world and modern-day situations.
Learning Outcomes:
Explain the political, social, and economic forces behind the emergence of the modern U.S. state, contextualizing historical events by tracing continuity and change over time.
Recognize the causes and effects of urbanization, industrialization, and westward expansion in the late 19th century and the tools and analytical lenses that historians use to research and interpret the past.
Analyze how World War II, the Cold War, and the civil rights revolution transformed the United States in the mid-20th century by considering causation, context, and complexity.
Interpret recent U.S. history by connecting past debates over government, economy, and culture to contemporary domestic and foreign policy conflicts.
