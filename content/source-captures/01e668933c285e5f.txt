COURSE DESCRIPTION
Sophia Learning's English Composition II course is designed to build upon the concepts taught in English
Composition I by focusing on a research perspective. In this course, you will be introduced to research
techniques for academic writing and will practice navigating the steps of the research process. You'll start by
choosing a topic that is meaningful for you, and develop critical research and writing skills as you prepare to
complete a final 6-8 page argumentative research essay.
COURSE EFFECTIVE DATES: November 2025 - Present
PREREQUISITES: No prerequisites; prior completion of English Composition I is recommended.
LENGTH OF COURSE: This is a self-paced course. Students may use as much or as little time as needed to
complete the course.
ACE LEARNING EVALUATIONS RECOMMENDATION: In the lower-division baccalaureate category, 3 semester
hours in English Composition II (ACE ID: SOPH-0030).
GRADING: This is a pass/fail course. Students must complete 9 Challenges (formative assessments) and 7
Touchstones (project-based or written assessments) with an overall score of 70% or better.

Touchstones are projects that illustrate comprehension of the course material, help refine skills, and
demonstrate application of knowledge. Read further for information on the touchstones in this course:
Touchstone 1.1: Find a Credible Source (35 points): Students will use the critical thinking skills they learned
in this unit by finding a credible source. Students will practice those skills by determining why the source
they chose should be considered credible evidence and then reflecting on their process. The goal of this
touchstone is to help students get comfortable with locating and evaluating research.
Touchstone 1.2: Summarize and Respond to Research (35 points): Students will practice their critical
reading skills by summarizing and responding to the source they found in Touchstone 1.1. Students will
deepen their ability to engage with scholarly or credible information, build their analytical thinking, and
prepare for future academic writing (including their final paper for this course).
Touchstone 2.1: Write an Outline and Thesis Statement (50 points): Students will apply what they have
learned about planning the structure of an argumentative essay by developing a clear thesis and organizing
their ideas into a structured outline.
Touchstone 2.2: Create an Annotated Bibliography (50 points): Students will learn how to select credible
academic sources and plan how to use them in support of an argument. By completing this touchstone,
students will be better prepared to write a well-supported, ethical, and persuasive essay in Touchstone 3.

Touchstone 3.1 Analyze Academic Research (50 points): In this assignment, students will have a chance to
practice the key skills of analysis and synthesis, including the strategic use of evidence.
Touchstone 3.2: Draft Your Essay (100 points): Using your outline and annotated bibliography from
Touchstones 2.1 and 2.2, students will draft a 6-8 page argumentative research essay on their chosen topic.
Students will use persuasive writing strategies and incorporate credible evidence to support their thesis.
Touchstone 4: Final Revision of Your Essay (150 points): Students will apply revision, editing, and
proofreading strategies to improve their draft argumentative essay using feedback.

LEARNING OUTCOMES
Upon completion of the course, the student will be able to:
. Articulate the purpose and scope of academic writing.
. Outline an argumentative essay around a thesis statement and secondary academic resources.
. Complete a first draft of an argumentative essay that is persuasive, evidence-based, and well structured.
. Demonstrate revision, editing, and proofreading skills to refine and polish a draft into a final draft.
