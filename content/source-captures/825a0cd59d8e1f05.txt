Marketing degree courses

Requirements (40 credit hours)
MKTG 301 Foundations for Professional Success (4 credits)
This course prepares students for success in their academic and professional careers. The foundation for professional development is explained through an appraisal of the student's learning style, emotional intelligence, ethics, and self-management style. Students demonstrate best practices for academic research in their chosen field of study by examining current marketing trends, employment, and professional networking tools. Finally, the students practice communication strategies for groups, individual presentations, and professional networking tools.
MKTG 302 Information & Influence (4 credits)
This course is designed to equip students with the necessary skills and knowledge to communicate effectively in a fast-paced and competitive environment. Students will learn to use information and influence to achieve their business objectives while maintaining ethical standards. Course material will cover topics such as communication channels, message design, audience analysis, persuasion, and communication technology. Students will analyze and apply theories and concepts from communication studies, psychology, marketing, and business to develop effective communication strategies for various business contexts. Must be taken in order.
MKTG 306 Solving Marketing Problems (4 credits)
This course embraces the philosophy of problem-solving beginning with a clear comprehension of the real marketing problem, setting realistic goals, accessing needed resources, managers and employees that are committed to using critical thinking to problem solve marketing decisions. Participants will gain exposure to ethical marketing decision-making, which starts with the organization's top management and guides the rest of the workforce on how to conduct their professional behavior. Students explore critical components of the business environment that impact organizational success and gain understanding of the data sources and analytical tools that assist in the decision-making process. Must be taken in order.
MKTG 308 Enhancing Fiscal Performance Through Responsible Marketing (4 credits)
This course focuses on the skills required to understand financial data and reliable tools that drive decision making within the organization. The transparency of data from the financial management system allows marketing leaders to better control the decision on who the optimum business or customer clients to pursue. Amass an appreciation of marketing math through changes to pricing policy of individual products as well as the larger product mix. Students will gain an overview of the practical application of accounting concepts and processes that relate to the overall economic health of the organization. Finally, value what is meant by securing and maintaining long-term profitable customer relationships that minimize risk. Must be taken in order
MKTG 402 Marketing Dynamics (4 credits)
This course provides a comprehensive introduction to the field of marketing. It covers the current marketing concepts and practical applications that will include the functions of product, price, place, promotion, and positioning. Participants will receive an introduction to the fundamentals of customer communications, sales presentations, graphic arts, and/or like-minded promotional messaging. Students are introduced to the holistic marketing approach, focusing on the development, creation and implementation of marketing activities within an organization. Must be taken in order.
MKTG 406 Data for Decision Making (4 credits)
This course is designed to provide students with the necessary skills and knowledge necessary to use data effectively in marketing decision-making. Students will learn how to collect, analyze, and interpret data to make informed decisions about marketing strategies and tactics to improve performance. Course materials will cover topics such as market research, data collection methods, data analysis techniques, and data visualization. Students will also learn about statistical inference and hypothesis testing, which are essential tools for making data-driven decisions. Must be taken in order.
MKTG 408 Identifying Customers (4 credits)
This course introduces consumer behavior, with an emphasis on developing a customer focus and competitive advantage by using consumer behavior analysis. Students expand upon their knowledge of market research and its application in identifying market segments and building effective customer relationships. The goal is to understand what effects stemming from internal and external influences affect the consumer decision making process in the marketplace. Identifying customer behaviors leads to designing products/services to meet their needs and wants consistent with the different consumer characteristics found in the marketplace. Must be taken in order.
MKTG 416 Customer Touchpoints (4 credits)
This course is designed to provide students with a comprehensive understanding of pinpointing marketing channels and engineering target audiences. Students will complete the process of identifying, understanding and engaging audiences through the use of data-driven insights, segmentation, and personalized messaging. It involves analyzing customer behavior, preferences, and demographics to create a more accurate profile of the target audience and develop strategies that appeal to their specific needs and interests. Students will learn how to develop and implement marketing strategies that effectively reach and engage target audiences across various channels. Must be taken in order.
MKTG 418 Social Media (4 credits)
This course is designed to provide students with an understanding and application of social media marketing. Students will learn how to develop and implement effective social media strategies that engage target audiences and drive business success. The course will cover topics such as leveraging social media platforms, content creation, community management, influencer marketing, social media advertising, and social media analytics. Students will also learn about the latest trends and innovations. Must be taken in order.
MKTG 422 Contemporary Marketing Skills (4 credits)
This special topic course is designed to provide students with an in-depth exploration of specific areas of marketing that are not covered in the regular program curriculum. The course will focus on current topics or emerging trends in marketing and will be taught by faculty members who are experts in the field.
The course will cover a range of topics related to the special topic, including the history and evolution of the topic, the current state of the industry, and future trends and developments. Students will also examine case studies and real-world examples to gain a deeper understanding of how the topic is being applied in the marketplace.
The specific topics covered in this course will vary depending on the semester. Must be taken in order.

How long does it typically take to finish a degree?
The time it takes to finish a degree depends on factors such as transfer credits, course load, and whether you study full-time or part-time. Many adults pace their studies around work or family responsibilities, starting slowly and adjusting their load as they gain momentum. Students with previous credits may progress more quickly, while first-time learners may prefer a steadier pace. Estimating your timeline usually begins by reviewing past coursework, your weekly availability, and the pace that feels realistic for your situation.
Can I work full-time while earning a degree?
Working full-time while earning a degree is possible, and the best approach depends on your weekly schedule and course expectations. Many adults begin with one course to understand the workload before increasing their pace. Planning ahead, creating a weekly routine, and staying organized can help maintain steady progress. Flexible course structures often make it easier for working learners to balance school with professional and personal responsibilities.
How do transfer credits work when returning to finish a degree?
Transfer credits are evaluated by comparing your previous coursework to current program requirements to see where past classes may still apply. An evaluation helps returning learners understand which credits remain usable and what requirements still need to be met. This clarity supports adults in planning their remaining academic pathway.
What is online learning like compared to taking classes on campus?
Online learning is a flexible format where you complete coursework through weekly modules instead of attending scheduled class meetings. Most courses include readings, assignments, recorded lectures, and discussion activities that keep learners engaged. Because participation is asynchronous, you can move through material at times that fit your schedule. This structure appeals to adults balancing school with work or family commitments while still offering consistent instructor guidance.
How do I know which degree program is the best fit for my goals?
You can determine which degree fits your goals by reflecting on your interests, long-term plans, and the types of work you enjoy. Reviewing course descriptions and learning outcomes can highlight how programs differ. Many adults also consider past experiences or strengths that feel energizing or meaningful. Speaking with advisors or professionals may offer additional insight. A good-fit program usually feels both engaging and aligned with the future you envision.
Will earning a degree help me stay competitive in the job market?
A degree may help you stay competitive by strengthening transferable skills such as communication, analysis, and problem-solving. Coursework often introduces concepts relevant across many industries and helps learners understand organizational expectations. While advancement depends on several factors, continued learning can help adults stay adaptable as workplace needs and technologies evolve.
What kinds of skills do most degree programs help students develop?
Most degree programs help students develop analytical thinking, communication, and problem-solving skills. Coursework teaches learners to interpret information, evaluate options, and apply concepts in practical situations. Many programs also emphasize collaboration, digital literacy, and effective written communication. These broad competencies support adaptability and help graduates contribute in a variety of professional environments.
What support is available for students returning to school after a long break?
Students returning after a long break can access support such as tutoring, writing assistance, academic advising, and time-management tools. Many adults begin with one course to rebuild study habits and reestablish confidence. These resources help learners ease back into academic expectations while balancing school with work and family responsibilities.
Are online degrees respected by employers?
Online degrees are generally respected by employers when they come from accredited institutions and are backed by strong coursework and demonstrated skills. Most employers focus on a candidate’s abilities, experience, and readiness to contribute rather than the format in which the degree was completed. Accreditation, curriculum quality, and faculty expertise typically matter more than delivery method.
How can a degree add value beyond career opportunities?
A degree can add personal value by strengthening confidence, broadening perspective, and supporting long-term goals. Many adults find that coursework provides structure, momentum, and opportunities to master new concepts. Completing assignments and meeting academic expectations can reinforce resilience and a sense of progress. Education often contributes to personal development in ways that extend beyond career advancement.
