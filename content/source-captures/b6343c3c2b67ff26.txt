Empower Your Future with Information Technology Expertise
Champlain College’s online Bachelor’s in Information Technology prepares you to excel in today’s evolving tech landscape. This flexible, transfer-friendly program is designed to build critical thinking, adaptability, and technical skills across networking, programming, cybersecurity, and emerging technologies—everything you need to succeed in a fast-paced IT career.
Finish your program faster with credit for prior learning and experience .
Differentiate your degree and get your resume noticed with an added certificate or graduate courses . Speak with your advisor for information.
Consider a career using the skills you learn in this degree.
Program Highlights
Networking and Architecture Mastery: Learn to design and implement robust networking solutions to support organizational goals, equipping you to manage complex IT infrastructures.
Comprehensive Programming Skills: Develop and deploy software using multiple programming languages and paradigms, building versatility in software and application development.
Cybersecurity Fundamentals: Gain hands-on experience in identifying and mitigating cybersecurity threats to safeguard information systems and organizational data.
Web and Server Administration Proficiency: Acquire skills in web development and server management to create dynamic, data-driven websites and manage server environments.
Innovation with Emerging Technologies: Evaluate and implement new technologies, including IoT and cloud computing, to solve real-world IT challenges.

Bachelor's to Master's Accelerated Pathway
Thinking about a master’s degree? As an information technology bachelor’s student, you can take up to 9 graduate credits within your general electives, billed at the undergraduate tuition rate, and those credits count toward your master’s so you finish sooner and spend less. Use those credits to earn a graduate certificate aligned with your intended master’s, or, if no certificate fits, take the first courses from your master’s program. Visit the Bachelor's to Masters Pathway page for details, and connect with your academic success advisor early to make sure your courses apply.

Requirements for the Information Technology Bachelor's Degree
Our admissions team seeks to admit students who:
Demonstrate a solid academic foundation - a minimum 2.5 GPA is our recommendation, though exceptions may be made on a case-by-case basis for those who demonstrate a potential for academic success in other ways.

Exhibit the ability to make a positive contribution to the Champlain College Online community.

Time to Completion
A 120-credit bachelor's degree in information technology traditionally takes 4 years to complete. Your time to completion is dependent on how many courses you take per term and whether you have credits to transfer.
CCO accepts up to 90 transfer credits toward a bachelor's degree. If you transfer the maximum, you could have as few as 10 courses (about 5 terms) left to finish. The average CCO bachelor's student brings in 40 credits. That leaves about 80 credits, or 27 courses, to complete. If you take two classes per 7-week term , that works out to just over 2 years on average to earn a bachelor’s degree . Your own timeline may be shorter or longer, depending on your situation.
We try to make online learning as seamless and quick as you want it to be. Because we believe, no matter where you've started, CCO is where you can finish strong – and save time and tuition along the way.

About
Daniel Selicaro is an Assistant Professor and Program Director for Technology at Champlain College Online. He oversees bachelor degree programs for Software Development, Web Design and Development, and Computer Science, as well as the graduate program in Software Engineering and Project Management.
Dan has been at Champlain College since 2007, and has been teaching web design and development for Champlain since 2015. He began his career in print journalism as a newspaper reporter, and was the news editor for Upside magazine and the Red Herring magazine in California. After moving back to the East Coast, he worked on digital marketing at Champlain College, and was an active part of two redevelopments of the College website, champlain.edu, before he took a role in the Information Systems department.
Outside of Champlain, Dan runs his own website development company, and is on the board of directors for several local organizations — including as marketing chair at the Fellowship of the Wheel mountain biking club, the Green Mountain Montessori School, and the Essex United Soccer Club. He is an avid mountain biker, and loves to spend his time adventuring in Vermont with his two young children.

Frequently Asked Questions
Following are some of the questions we are asked about our bachelor's degree in information technology.
Do I need IT experience to enroll in this degree program?
No prior experience is required. The program is described as flexible and transfer-friendly, and coursework builds foundational skills from courses like Networking Fundamentals and Introduction to Python.
What technical skills will I develop through this program?
This IT bachelor's degree program covers networking and architecture design, programming across multiple languages and paradigms (Python, C++, PHP, shell scripting), cybersecurity threat identification and mitigation, web development and server administration (Windows and Linux), and emerging technologies including blockchain, IoT, edge computing, and cloud architecture.
This is an online program, will I get any hands-on experience?
Yes — the curriculum is built around hands-on labs and projects throughout, such as building password-manager programs in Python, designing and populating databases in SQL, configuring Windows and Linux servers, and building PHP/MySQL-driven websites.
How many courses can I take each term?
As you pursue your associate degree in data science, you may take 1 course, or you may take 2-3 courses per each 7-week term. However, you may not take more than three courses (9 credit hours) in a term, or 6 courses (18 credit hours) in a 15-week semester without prior program director approval. As always, consult your Student Success Advisor with any questions.
