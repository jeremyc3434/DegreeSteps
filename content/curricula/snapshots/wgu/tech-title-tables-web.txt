L307@P8: Cybersecurity and Ethics
L308@P8: When considering a major in cybersecurity, students must understand the importance of ethics and
L309@P8: principles such as integrity, responsibility, and respect for privacy. Ethical behavior helps prevent misuse of
L310@P8: critical data and fosters trust with clients, employers, and the public. This trust is essential for effective
L311@P8: collaboration and security measures. Ethical behavior also guides professionals in making decisions that
L312@P8: comply with laws and regulations, preventing breaches, legal issues, or damage to an organization's
L313@P8: reputation. Maintaining high ethical standards is key to building a secure and reliable digital environment.
L314@P8: While there are no WGU-specific criteria related to an applicant’s background for program participation,
L315@P8: some professional organizations like ISC2 require you to disclose any criminal history, association with
L316@P8: criminal computer activity, or loss of license or certification during their application process. This may impact
L317@P8: your ability to obtain specific certifications that align with WGU’s standards of competency. Maintaining a
L318@P8: clean legal record is important as it can affect your opportunities for certification and future employment
L319@P8: opportunities in the field.
L320@P8: Cybersecurity is not a regulated profession; however, employers often require thorough background checks
L321@P8: to ensure candidates have a history of integrity and reliability. Depending on the job, you might also need a
L322@P8: security clearance, especially if you'll be working with sensitive government data. Obtaining a clearance
L323@P8: involves a detailed review of your personal, financial, and criminal history. Any criminal records can impact
L324@P8: your chances of getting these clearances.
