L23@P1: Understanding	 	 the Competency-Based Approach
L24@P1: How do competency-based programs like those offered at Western Governors University (WGU) work?
L25@P1: Unlike traditional universities, WGU does not award degrees based on completing a certain number of
L26@P1: credit hours or a specific set of required courses. Instead, you will earn your degree by demonstrating your
L27@P1: skills, knowledge, and understanding of essential concepts.
L28@P1: Progress through a degree program is measured not by the amount of time you spend in class but by your
L29@P1: ability to demonstrate competency as you complete required courses along a Standard Path. To help you
L30@P1: acquire the knowledge and skills you need to demonstrate competency and complete your courses and
L31@P1: program, WGU provides a rich array of learning resources. Your program mentor will work closely with you
L32@P1: to help you understand your program's requirements and help you create a plan for completing your
L33@P1: courses. You will also work closely with course instructors as you engage in each course. As subject
L34@P1: matter experts, course instructors will guide you through the content you must learn to demonstrate
L35@P1: competency through the course assessments.
L36@P1: The benefit of this competency-based system is that it enables students who are knowledgeable about a
L37@P1: particular subject to make accelerated progress toward completing a degree, even if they lack college
L38@P1: experience. You may have gained skills and knowledge of a subject while on the job, accumulated wisdom
L39@P1: through years of life experience, or already taken a course on a particular subject. WGU will award your
L40@P1: degree based on the skills and knowledge you possess and can demonstrate—not the number of hours
L41@P1: spent in a classroom.

L50@P1: The Degree Plan
L51@P1: The focus of your program is your personalized Degree Plan. The Degree Plan is a detailed blueprint of the
L52@P1: courses you will need to complete in order to earn your degree. The Degree Plan also lays out the
L53@P1: accompanying learning resources and assessments that compose your program. The list of courses in the
L54@P1: Degree Plan is often referred to as the standard path. The amount of time it takes to complete your
L55@P1: program depends on both the amount of new information you need to learn and the amount of time you
L56@P1: plan to devote each week to study. Your program mentor and course instructors will help you assess your
L57@P1: strengths and development needs to establish a study plan.
L58@P1: Students vary widely in the specific skills and information they need to learn. For example, some students
L59@P1: may be highly knowledgeable in a particular subject matter and would not need to engage in new learning
L60@P1: opportunities. Other students may find that portions of the program require them to learn new information
L61@P1: and that they need to take an online class or participate in a study module to acquire the knowledge and
L62@P1: skills needed to fulfill program competencies in that area. Some individuals may be able to devote as little
L63@P1: as 15–20 hours per week to the program, while others may need to devote more time. For this reason,
L64@P1: pre-assessments are there to help your program mentor form a profile of your prior knowledge and create
L65@P1: a personalized Degree Plan.

L80@P1: How You Will Interact with Faculty
L81@P1: At WGU, faculty serve in specialized roles, and they will work with you individually to provide the guidance,
L82@P1-2: instruction, and support you will need to succeed and graduate. As a student, it is important for you to take
L83@P2: advantage of this support. It is key to your progress and ultimate success. Upon your enrollment, you will
L84@P2: be assigned a program mentor—an expert in your field of study who will provide you with regular
L85@P2: program-level guidance and support from the day you start until the day you graduate. Your program
L86@P2: mentor will set up regular telephone appointments (weekly at first) with you, which you will be expected to
L87@P2: keep. The mentor will review program competencies with you and work with you to develop a plan and
L88@P2: schedule for your coursework. Your program mentor will serve as your main point of contact throughout
L89@P2: your program—helping you set weekly study goals, recommending specific learning materials, telling you
L90@P2: what to expect in courses, and keeping you motivated. In addition to regular calls, your program mentor is
L91@P2: available to help you resolve questions and concerns as they arise.
L92@P2: You will also be assigned to a course instructor for each course. Course instructors are doctoral-level
L93@P2: subject matter experts who will assist your learning in each individual course. When you begin a new
L94@P2: course, your assigned course instructor will actively monitor your progress and will be in touch to offer
L95@P2: one-on-one instruction and to provide you with information about webinars, cohort sessions, and other
L96@P2: learning opportunities available to help you acquire the competencies you need to master the course. Your
L97@P2: course instructor can discuss your learning for the course, help you find answers to content questions, and
L98@P2: give you the tools to navigate the course successfully. In addition, you will communicate with course
L99@P2: instructors by posting in the online learning community and participating in live discussion sessions such as
L100@P2: webinars and cohorts.
L101@P2: For many of the courses at WGU, you will be required to complete performance assessments. These
L102@P2: include reports, papers, presentations, and projects that let you demonstrate your mastery of the required
L103@P2: competencies. A separate group of faculty members, called evaluators, will review your work to determine
L104@P2: whether it meets requirements. Evaluators are also subject matter experts in their field of evaluation. If your
L105@P2: assessment needs further work before it “passes,” these evaluators, who review your work anonymously,
L106@P2: will provide you with instructional feedback to help you meet evaluation standards and allow you to
L107@P2: advance.

L159@P3: Continuous Enrollment, On Time Progress, and Satisfactory Academic
L160@P3: Progress
L161@P3: WGU is a “continuous enrollment” institution, which means you will be automatically enrolled in each of
L162@P3: your new terms while you are at WGU. Each term is six months long. Longer terms and continuous
L163@P3: enrollment allow you to focus on your studies without the hassle of unnatural breaks between terms that
L164@P3: you would experience at a more traditional university. At the end of every six-month term, you and your
L165@P3: program mentor will review the progress you have made and revise your Degree Plan for your next sixmonth term.
L166@P3: WGU requires that students make measurable progress toward the completion of their degree programs
L167@P3: every term. We call this “On-Time Progress,” denoting that you are on track and making progress toward
L168@P3: on-time graduation. As full-time students, graduate students must enroll in at least 8 competency units
L169@P3: each term, and undergraduate students must enroll in at least 12 competency units each term. Completing
L170@P3: at least these minimum enrollments is essential to On-Time Progress and serves as a baseline from which
L171@P3: you may accelerate your program. We measure your progress based onthe courses you are able to pass,
L172@P3: not on your accumulation of credit hours or course grades. Every time you pass a course, you are
L173@P3: demonstrating that you have mastered skills and knowledge in your degree program. For comparison to
L174@P3: traditional grading systems, passing a course means you have demonstrated competency equivalent to a
L175@P3: “B” grade or better.
L176@P3: WGU assigns competency units to each course in order to track your progress through the program. A
L177@P3: competency unit is equivalent to one semester credit of learning. Some courses may be assigned 3
L178@P3: competency units while others may be as large as 12 competency units.
