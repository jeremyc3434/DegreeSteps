## starting-route
Title: What a plan's "starting route" means
Sources: /credits-worth.html, plan finder and guided planner copy
Each researched degree plan opens on its published starting route: a proposed choice for every degree requirement, using the course options that plan lists from the school's published sources. The finder, guided planner and worksheet quote the same starting-route numbers: the credits proposed from outside providers and the credits taken at the university. Your courses, course sources and priorities can change this. These are planned credits; your college makes the final decisions about your own transfer awards.
Links: /programs.html, /portal.html, /credits-worth.html

## outside-credit
Title: Outside credit
Sources: /faq/, /guides/how-transfer-credit-works/
Many schools consider credit from other colleges, approved exams, military learning, and courses with ACE or NCCRS recommendations. Acceptance and degree placement depend on the exact school, program, course, dates, and your individual evaluation. Acceptance and progress toward graduation are separate decisions: a course might satisfy a required subject, count as a general elective, or have no remaining space in your degree. Before paying for a subscription, exam, or university term, ask the school about the exact outside courses you want to use.
Links: /guides/how-transfer-credit-works/, /faq/

## ace-nccrs
Title: ACE and NCCRS credit recommendations
Sources: /faq/, /guides/ace-nccrs-and-accreditation/
ACE and NCCRS recommendations can help a college evaluate learning completed outside a traditional college classroom. An ACE recommendation does not guarantee transfer credit: ACE makes credit recommendations; it does not accredit a provider as a college or require schools to accept a course. The receiving school decides whether the learning is accepted and which degree requirements it satisfies. Some schools apply a combined limit to selected types of recommended learning. Ask a concrete question: "For my degree and catalog year, would this provider's course with this identifier and completion date satisfy this requirement?"
Links: /guides/ace-nccrs-and-accreditation/

## needs-school-confirmation
Title: Plans that "need school confirmation"
Sources: plan finder, guided planner, worksheet and transfer pages
The plan finder lists reviewed plans and complete plans that still need school confirmation; each card is labelled. A plan that needs school confirmation has a complete proposed course plan, but it still has open questions for the school, listed on the plan as "Ask the school" items. Ask the school those questions before you pay for courses. Transfer choices still need school confirmation; your school decides what transfers.
Links: /programs.html

## planning-estimate
Title: Planning estimates and "known charges so far"
Sources: /credits-worth.html, plan finder and worksheet
Planning estimates use the same reviewed rates as the plan worksheet: tuition, fees and outside course prices before financial aid. When a plan has any price we haven't reviewed, we say the cost needs confirmation instead of guessing. When some selected course prices are not included yet, the range is shown as "known charges so far", never as a total. A reliable final price also requires current school fees, approved credits, your pace, and actual course schedules. Courses, tuition and provider fees are separate; DegreeSteps is free to explore.
Links: /programs.html, /faq/

## residency
Title: University residency
Sources: /blog/what-a-75-percent-transfer-limit-means/, /guides/how-transfer-credit-works/
University residency is an academic requirement about coursework or approved learning through the institution. It should not be confused with whether you live in the state or attend a campus. Programs can combine an overall transfer limit with required university coursework, upper-level study, and major conditions. Ask which credits must come from the university and whether a major has additional restrictions.
Links: /blog/what-a-75-percent-transfer-limit-means/, /guides/how-transfer-credit-works/

## proctoring
Title: Proctoring and exam fees
Sources: /guides/finish-faster-without-wasting-credit/, credit source pages
Assessment and proctoring requirements depend on the course, and examinations may need appointments. A full remaining budget includes outside subscriptions or individual courses, assessments, proctoring, labs and records. CLEP test-center or remote-proctor administration fees vary and are not included.
Links: /guides/finish-faster-without-wasting-credit/
